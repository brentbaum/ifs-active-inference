1. Central Thesis

Parts are modular subgraphs in a single generative model's factor graph. IFS therapeutic change is the progressive reconnection of these subgraphs to context — a graph-topological operation, not a parametric one.

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is an empirically supported psychotherapy model built on the idea that the psyche contains semi-autonomous "parts" — exiles, managers, firefighters — organized around a core "Self." Despite growing clinical evidence, IFS lacks a formal mechanistic account. This draft provides one using the language of active inference.

The key move is to interpret parts not as separate agents (the clinical metaphor) or as mere high-precision priors (too simple), but as disconnected subgraphs in the generative model's factor graph. This single structural property — modularity — explains both why parts persist unchanged (no prediction errors reach them) and why they dominate when activated (they don't condition on context).

For readers unfamiliar with the clinical framework:

3. The Model: Parts as Disconnected Subgraphs

A part is a cluster of associated priors within a single generative model, together with a precision hyperprior (how strongly those priors dominate inference) and an activation prior (when the cluster activates based on context matching). Crucially, an exile's subgraph lacks edges to context variables.

Figure 1. The core mechanism. Left: an exile's subgraph S is disconnected from context C — no messages reach it, so its priors cannot update ("frozen"). Right: re-contextualization adds edges between S and C, enabling prediction errors to flow. This single graph-topological change simultaneously makes the schema explicit, embeds it in context, and restores plasticity.
Part := { associated_priors: [p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ], // subset of generative model precision_hyperprior: π_part, // how strongly to weight these activation_prior: P("I am needed" | context) // when to activate } // A part activates when posterior over hidden states // matches the region the subgraph was formed to handle — // including interoceptive state (body is part of the world model).

4. Blending: How a Part Captures Inference

Blending occurs when a modular subgraph captures policy selection. Because the subgraph lacks edges to context variables, it generates context-blind predictions that win over context-dependent alternatives. A positive feedback loop reinforces this: activation increases precision on the part's priors, which further suppresses competing signals.

Figure 2. The blending cascade. A modular subgraph, lacking edges to context, wins inferential competition and captures policy selection. Disconfirming evidence cannot propagate into the subgraph. Downstream, protector policies activate: firefighters (reactive, low temporal depth) and managers (proactive, high temporal depth).

Why blending is self-reinforcing: The modular subgraph has no incoming edges for disconfirming evidence. Its predictions are self-consistent within its closed loop. Breaking blending requires external intervention — a therapist, Self-energy, or environmental change — that can bypass the modularity.

5. Protectors: Policies Over Different Horizons

Figure 3. Protectors differ in temporal depth. Firefighters minimize acute free energy (immediate reaction when exile breaks through). Managers plan trajectories to prevent exile activation entirely. Both exist because exile priors are frozen attractors that hijack inference when activated.

6. Self as Default Inference

Self is not a part and does not need explicit modeling. It is what Bayesian inference naturally produces when no modular subgraph dominates. The 8 C's are default properties of a fully connected generative model.
Figure 4. Each of IFS's "8 C's" of Self maps to a computational property that Bayesian inference naturally exhibits when no modular subgraph distorts it. Testable prediction: these qualities should co-occur and all increase as modularity decreases.

7. Two Mechanisms of Therapeutic Change

The draft's most distinctive theoretical move is collapsing IFS's apparent three mechanisms of change into two. "Welcoming" (making an exile's experience conscious) and "unburdening" (releasing extreme beliefs) are the same operation — re-contextualization — at different depths of modularity.

Mechanism 1: Self-Energy Increase

Operation: Raise global precision ceiling (πmax)

What changes: System-wide parameter. No individual part can achieve winner-take-all dominance.

Role: Enables the work. You can't do graph surgery while fully blended.

Mechanism 2: Re-contextualization

Operation: Add edges between isolated subgraph S and context network C (structure learning)

What changes: Accessibility, context-embedding, AND plasticity — simultaneously.

Role: IS the work. This is what both "welcoming" and "unburdening" describe.

"Frozen = disconnected." A prior is non-updating not because of a special plasticity lock, but because no prediction errors can reach it. Re-contextualization (adding edges) IS unfreezing. The "closed learning window" is a consequence of graph topology, not a separate property.

Depth Spectrum

If welcoming and unburdening are the same mechanism, what explains the clinical variation? Depth of modularity, governed by consolidation strength and hierarchical wrapping:

Figure 5. What varies is depth, not kind. Shallow modularity resolves on reconnection alone — the prediction error from contact with current context is sufficient. Deeper consolidation requires vivid juxtaposition. Identity-level wrapping requires reconnection at a higher level of the factor graph.

Why this unification matters

It explains several otherwise-puzzling clinical phenomena:

  • Witnessing must precede unburdening — you can't send messages to a disconnected node. Integration is prerequisite because integration IS the mechanism.
  • Some parts unburden spontaneously during witnessing (~50%) — shallow modularity resolves on reconnection alone.
  • Unburdening is quick but integration takes time — reconnection is a discrete graph topology change; belief updating through new edges requires subsequent experience.
  • Protectors relax after exile unburdening — the exile's beliefs are now normal updatable priors, no longer dangerous frozen attractors.
  • Parts "look different" after — a reconnected subgraph gets reorganized by information flowing through it.

10. Status & Open Questions

What's here

  • Core conceptual framework with factor-graph interpretation
  • Translation of all major IFS constructs into active inference
  • Narrative example (dog phobia, before/during/after)
  • Logical decomposition into axioms, definitions, claims, and inferences
  • Validated Julia implementations of related models (Chamberlin coherence therapy, Smith spider phobia)

What's not yet here

  • Mathematical formalization (Section 6 is placeholder — no equations yet)
  • Simulation of the IFS model itself
  • Falsifiability loop (phenomenology list is most promising direction)
  • Precision taxonomy (the draft overloads "precision" across 4+ meanings)
  • Resolution of tension between working thesis ("precision-modulating meta-priors") and developed argument ("modular subgraphs")

Key open questions

  1. Why attunement, not just knowledge? Cognitive acknowledgment doesn't heal; felt resonance does. Hypothesis: attunement creates edges to interoceptive/affective nodes where the part actually lives, with the right precision profile to interface with a somatic subgraph.
  2. Partial witnessing. Parts can be partially seen. Is this partial reconnection (some edges but not all), low-precision edges (weak messages), or reconnection at one hierarchical level but not another?
  3. Self vs. dissociation. Both are "quiet." How to formally distinguish Self-led calm (curiosity + capacity to stay with distress) from protective quietness (numbing, dissociation)?
  4. Therapist modeling. Should the therapist be modeled explicitly as a second agent in dyadic active inference?
  5. Predictive validation. Can we predict from pre-therapy measures which cases will resolve with Discovery alone vs. requiring explicit unburdening?
