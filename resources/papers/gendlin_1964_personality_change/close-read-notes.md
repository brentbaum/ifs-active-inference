# Gendlin (1964) "A Theory of Personality Change" — close-read notes (2026-07-06)

Citation: Gendlin, E. T. (1964). A theory of personality change. In P. Worchel & D. Byrne (Eds.), *Personality change* (pp. 100–148). Wiley. Online: https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2145.html (cite by Gendlin's § numbers/headings — stable across editions; original Wiley pagination only in print).

## Verbatim quotes verified (quote these, not the paraphrases)

- **"Frozen wholes"** — HIS term, §13 subsection heading. "my experience is a 'frozen whole' and will not give up its structure." Cognate: "frozen structure" (§13, §19). **"Frozen process" is NOT Gendlin's phrase — it is our coinage (via Aletheia framing). Adapt, never quote as his.** In his usage process is what's *absent* when frozen.
- **Structure-bound state** (§13, "Richness of Fresh Detail"): "I experience only this bare outline and feel only this bare set of emotions, lacking the myriad of fresh detail of the present." Also: the present becomes "merely an occasion, a cue for a familiar, repetitious, structured pattern of feeling" (§13, Presentness).
- **Mechanism** (§13, "Repetitive versus Modifiable" — strongest single cite): "Since within the bare structured frozen whole experiencing does not function in interaction with present detail, the structure is not modified by the present. Hence, it remains the same, it repeats itself in many situations without ever changing."
- **Definition** (§14): structure-bound aspects "are not in process"; the process is "missing"; "only the process-skipping structure."

## The gold: relational carrying-forward (cite in §7 — C3's 1964 ancestor)

- §15 (Reconstituting): "Responses can reconstitute the experiencing process in respects in which, before the response, there was no process." ("events" explicitly includes "others' behaviors" — §15.)
- §19 (Self-Process): "the individual cannot respond to himself and carry forward his experiencing… responses are needed first to reconstitute the interaction process." Why the signal must come from outside the frozen aspect.
- §20: "One must respond to the functioning experiencing, not to the structure." = "you don't unfreeze structure by arguing with it." The corrective evidence enters through what still functions — maps onto relational PE reaching the part via the live relational channel rather than the frozen threat channel.
- §21: change happens *in* the interaction ("The changes are already occurring as he speaks").
- Relationship-section intro: "If we are sad and dry-eyed alone, then with this person we cry."
- IFS refinement over Gendlin: he says the individual cannot respond to himself in structure-bound respects; IFS's claim is that *Self* can — because "another" is bundle-relative, not skin-relative. Self stands outside the bundle even though inside the person. (Good §7 sentence.)

## Frictions (own these in the paper)

1. **Anti-content stance (biggest):** Gendlin denies experience is made of stored contents/beliefs ("Implicit bodily feeling is preconceptual"; the "Content Paradigm" critique; §16 "contents are process aspects"). Rendering the frozen whole as a high-precision prior with propositional content is OUR reconstruction — flag as translation (done in draft §3).
2. **Body substrate:** his implicit is "a sensing of body life"; precision-weighting is not his idiom.
3. **Frozen is a manner, not an entity:** "in this regard, my experience is a 'frozen whole'" — local property of process, not a discrete object. Relevant to parts-as-entities framing.
