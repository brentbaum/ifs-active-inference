# Logical Structure: Deane, Miller & Wilkinson (2020) — "Losing Ourselves"

Extracted from: *Losing Ourselves: Active Inference, Depersonalization, and Meditation*
Frontiers in Psychology, 29 October 2020

## Axioms (Imported from Prior Work)

### A1 — Free Energy Principle
Biological systems maintain existence by minimizing variational free energy, which measures the disattunement between agent and environment.
- **Depends on:** (foundational)
- **Source:** Friston 2010; Friston & Stephan 2007
- **Section:** From the Free Energy Principle to Hierarchical Predictive Processing

### A2 — Hierarchical Predictive Processing
The brain minimizes free energy via hierarchical generative models that issue top-down predictions and pass up prediction error.
- **Depends on:** A1
- **Source:** Friston 2005; Clark 2013, 2015
- **Section:** From the Free Energy Principle to Hierarchical Predictive Processing

### A3 — Precision Weighting
The brain learns context-dependent second-order statistics (precision) that modulate the gain on prediction error signals; this implements attention and arbitrates between competing hypotheses.
- **Depends on:** A2
- **Source:** Hohwy 2012
- **Section:** From the Free Energy Principle to Hierarchical Predictive Processing

### A4 — Active Inference
Prediction error can be minimized not only by updating the model (perceptual inference) but also by acting on the world to change sensory input (active inference).
- **Depends on:** A1, A2
- **Source:** Friston et al. 2017
- **Section:** From the Free Energy Principle to Hierarchical Predictive Processing

### A5 — Allostasis as Prospective Control
Organisms anticipate future dyshomeostatic conditions and proactively act to avoid them (allostasis), formalized through active inference.
- **Depends on:** A4
- **Source:** Sterling 2012; Corcoran et al. 2019; Pezzulo et al. 2015
- **Section:** A Control-Theoretic Perspective

### A6 — Temporally Deep Generative Models
Higher levels of the cortical hierarchy track regularities on longer timescales; policy selection (sequences of actions) is evaluated by expected free energy over temporal depth.
- **Depends on:** A2, A4
- **Source:** Kiebel et al. 2008; Friston et al. 2017; Pezzulo et al. 2015
- **Section:** A Control-Theoretic Perspective

### A7 — Pragmatic and Epistemic Value
Active inference balances pragmatic value (fulfilling prior preferences) and epistemic value (reducing uncertainty / information gain) in action selection.
- **Depends on:** A4, A6
- **Source:** Kaplan & Friston 2018; Friston et al. 2015
- **Section:** A Control-Theoretic Perspective

### A8 — Inferential Self-Models
The sense of self arises inferentially within a hierarchical generative model.
- **Depends on:** A2
- **Source:** Limanowski & Blankenburg 2013; Seth 2013; Apps & Tsakiris 2014; Allen & Friston 2016
- **Section:** The Sense of Self as a Model of Allostatic Control

### A9 — Comparator Model of Agency
The sense of agency arises from comparing predicted sensory consequences of actions with actual sensory consequences; attribution to endogenous (self) vs exogenous (world) causes follows from this comparison.
- **Depends on:** A4
- **Source:** Frith 2014; Haggard 2017; Miall & Wolpert 1996
- **Section:** Agentive Control

### A10 — Affective Valence Tracks Error Dynamics
Affective valence (positive/negative hedonic tone) reflects the rate of change in error management over time relative to expectations ("error dynamics").
- **Depends on:** A1, A3
- **Source:** Joffily & Coricelli 2013; Kiverstein et al. 2017; Van de Cruys 2017; Hesp et al. 2019
- **Section:** Affect in Deep Self-Models

### A11 — Phenomenal Transparency
A mental process is "transparent" insofar as its contents are available to consciousness while its constructive process is not; transparent states present their content as irrevocably real and personally essential.
- **Depends on:** (foundational — phenomenology)
- **Source:** Metzinger 2003, 2017
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### A12 — Depersonalization as Affective Numbing (Prior PP Accounts)
Existing predictive processing accounts (Seth et al. 2012; Gerrans 2019) explain depersonalization as resulting from pathologically imprecise interoceptive predictions, leading to loss of "presence."
- **Depends on:** A2, A3
- **Source:** Seth et al. 2012; Gerrans 2019
- **Section:** Existing Predictive Processing Accounts of Depersonalization

### A13 — Buddhist Doctrine of No-Self (Anatta)
The ordinary sense of a single, essential, enduring subject is a construction arising from impersonal psychophysical processes; recognizing this constructedness is the insight sought through meditation.
- **Depends on:** (foundational — Buddhist philosophy)
- **Source:** Hamilton 2000; Shulman 2014; Davis & Thompson 2017; Albahari 2014
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### A14 — Craving (Tanha) as Generator of Self-Illusion
In Buddhist theory, craving -- the felt urgency to make the world conform to desires -- unifies impersonal psychophysical processes under the illusion of a persistent essential self, and is the primary source of suffering.
- **Depends on:** A13
- **Source:** Albahari 2014; Analayo 2009
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

## Definitions

### D1 — Allostatic Control Model (ACM)
The self-model cast as an inference about the system's endogenous causes of self-evidencing outcomes, decomposed into (a) an agentive control component (what the system can do) and (b) a motivational component (what the system wants, grounded in goal priors and interoception).
- **Depends on:** A4, A5, A6, A8
- **Source:** Deane 2020 (formalized here)
- **Section:** The Sense of Self as a Model of Allostatic Control

### D2 — Agentive Control Component
The inference the system makes about its own capacity to bring about sensory consequences through action, learned through past action-outcome contingencies and extended across temporal depth.
- **Depends on:** A9, A6, D1
- **Section:** Agentive Control

### D3 — Motivational Component
The goal priors (from phylogenetically endowed homeostatic setpoints to acquired deep goal hierarchies) that define what the system is motivated to bring about, grounded primarily in interoception.
- **Depends on:** A5, A6, D1
- **Section:** Motivation

### D4 — Error Dynamics
The rate at which prediction errors are being accommodated relative to prior expectations of that rate; the slope plotting speed of error reduction against expected speed.
- **Depends on:** A1, A10
- **Section:** Affect in Deep Self-Models

### D5 — Subjective Fitness
A higher-level inference in the generative model that scores the "fit" between the current action model and the world -- how well or poorly the system is reducing error over time relative to expectations.
- **Depends on:** D1, D4
- **Source:** Formalized in Hesp et al. 2019; applied here
- **Section:** The Sense of Self as a Model of Allostatic Control

### D6 — Opacification
The process by which normally transparent mental processes become "opaque" -- their constructive nature becomes available to consciousness -- through sustained meditative observation.
- **Depends on:** A11
- **Source:** Carter et al. 2005; Metzinger 2003
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### D7 — Selfless Experience
Diminished sense of self reported across depersonalization, meditation, and psychedelic states; the paper's umbrella term for the phenomenon to be explained.
- **Depends on:** (observational)
- **Section:** Introduction

## Claims (Novel Assertions)

### C1 — Self = Inference about Allostatic Control
The sense of self is fundamentally an inference about the system's endogenous control of self-evidencing outcomes, formalized as the ACM. This is what the self *is* computationally.
- **Depends on:** A8, A5, A4, D1
- **Section:** The Sense of Self as a Model of Allostatic Control

### C2 — Affect and Self-Modeling Are Coconstitutive
Minimal self-modeling and affect are not merely correlated but mechanistically intertwined: affect *is* the inference about the performance of the action model in bringing about self-evidencing outcomes.
- **Depends on:** C1, A10, D4, D5
- **Section:** Affect in Deep Self-Models

### C3 — Affect as Domain-General Precision Controller
Affective valence functions as a domain-general controller that tracks and assigns precision relative to changes in expected rates of error reduction (expected free energy reduction).
- **Depends on:** A3, A10, D4
- **Source:** Builds on Kiverstein et al. 2017; Hesp et al. 2019
- **Section:** Affect in Deep Self-Models

### C4 — Valence Inherently Tracks Control, Not Just Interoceptive State
Prior PP accounts (Seth et al. 2012) treat emotion as interoceptive *perceptual* inference. The paper claims affect essentially involves *active* inference and inherently carries valence tied to allostatic control (positive = control, negative = loss of control), not merely interoceptive prediction error.
- **Depends on:** C1, C2, A4, A10
- **Section:** Existing Predictive Processing Accounts of Depersonalization

### C5 — Depersonalization = Inferred Loss of Allostatic Control
Depersonalization results from the system inferring a global loss of allostatic control -- it ceases to posit itself as a causally efficacious controller of sensations across contexts.
- **Depends on:** C1, C2, C3
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### C6 — Depersonalization Cascade Mechanism
When sustained unresolvable prediction error (e.g., trauma) prevents error reduction through either perceptual or active inference, negative affect cascades upward through the hierarchy: precision drops at each level of the self-model, eventually "unraveling" the affective inference process and the sense of self with it.
- **Depends on:** C5, C3, A6, D4
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### C7 — Depersonalization Is Inherently Negatively Valenced
On the ACM, depersonalization is *intrinsically* negative (not merely appraised as negative after the fact), because it arises from loss of control, which is constitutively tied to negative valence.
- **Depends on:** C5, C2, C4
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### C8 — Phenomenal Flatness from Loss of Allostatic Control
The world loses "phenomenal depth" and ceases to solicit engagement in depersonalization because the system infers global loss of confidence in action policies -- nothing is motivationally salient.
- **Depends on:** C5, C3
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### C9 — Meditation Increases Endogenous Control over Precision
Through focused attention meditation, the system learns to endogenously control precision on goal priors by repeatedly reducing precision on distracting policies via inaction.
- **Depends on:** C1, A3, D3
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### C10 — Meditative Selflessness = Opacification, Not Damping
Meditative selflessness arises through opacification of normally transparent precision-setting processes (both goal priors and affective valence), not through damping or loss of affect.
- **Depends on:** C9, D6, A11, C2
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### C11 — Opacification Disrupts Self-Attribution
When the system observes precision allocations (e.g., itch-to-scratch) occurring independently of agentive engagement (due to inaction policy), the usual action-outcome contingency basis for self-inference is disrupted, and processes are increasingly experienced as non-essential to the self.
- **Depends on:** C10, D2, D6, A9
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### C12 — Craving Maps to Transparent Valence Driving Policy Selection
The Buddhist concept of craving (tanha) corresponds computationally to valence transparently (automatically) conditioning policy selection -- when valence is experienced as immediately real, it creates urgency to act.
- **Depends on:** A14, A11, C3, D6
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### C13 — Opacification of Valence Separates Liking from Wanting
Gaining endogenous control over precision on goal priors enables the system to decouple (dis)liking from (not) wanting, so that valence no longer automatically conditions craving/aversion.
- **Depends on:** C10, C12, C9
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### C14 — Depersonalization and Meditative Selflessness Are Computationally Opposite
Despite superficial phenomenological similarity, depersonalization (loss of control, affect damped) and meditative selflessness (gain of control, affect opacified but intact) are diametrically opposed with respect to allostatic control.
- **Depends on:** C5, C9, C10, C7
- **Section:** Conclusion

### C15 — Meditation Can Produce Depersonalization Under Specific Conditions
Meditating on overwhelmingly traumatic content while practicing inaction reproduces the conditions for depersonalization (sustained unresolvable error + inability to act), explaining adverse meditation outcomes.
- **Depends on:** C6, C9, C14
- **Section:** Conclusion

### C16 — Recovery from Depersonalization Requires Restoring Affect Before Meditative Insight Is Possible
Transitioning from depersonalization to meditative selflessness requires first restoring phenomenal access to affective responses, because opacification requires intact affective signals as input.
- **Depends on:** C5, C10, C14
- **Section:** Conclusion

## Inferences (Derived Consequences)

### I1 — Self-Modeling Explains Derealization Co-occurrence
Because the self-model structures engagement with the world (via precision on action policies), loss of the self-model (depersonalization) entails loss of motivational salience of the environment (derealization). The two are "two sides of the same coin."
- **Depends on:** C5, C8
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### I2 — Depression-Depersonalization Comorbidity
Major depression can be understood as a domain-general inference of loss of allostatic self-efficacy; when no alternative contexts remain (no temporally deep goal priors to realize), total loss of allostatic control manifests as depersonalization.
- **Depends on:** C5, C6, D5
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### I3 — Trauma Reliably Produces Depersonalization
Trauma (especially torture) produces massive prediction error that the system cannot resolve through either perceptual or active inference, triggering the depersonalization cascade.
- **Depends on:** C6
- **Section:** ACM Account of Depersonalization

### I4 — Domain-General Control Increase Explains Meditation's Positive Affect
The pervasive feelings of joy and peace characterizing long-term meditation correspond to the system inferring increased domain-general allostatic control from learning to endogenously set precision.
- **Depends on:** C9, C2, D5
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### I5 — New Higher-Order Policies Become Available After Opacification
Once precision-setting processes are opacified, the system can develop new higher-order policies about *how* precision is being set -- valence becomes information that can be used or not, rather than an automatic driver of behavior.
- **Depends on:** C10, C13, D6
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### I6 — Mindfulness Effectiveness for Addiction
Mindfulness-based interventions for addiction work by opacifying the craving-to-action link: the system learns that cravings are feelings that can be allowed to direct behavior or not.
- **Depends on:** I5, C12, C13
- **Section:** ACM Account of Meditative Selflessness

### I7 — Hyperreflective Body-Checking in DPD as Compensatory Behavior
The hyperreflective tendency to check one's body common in depersonalization can be understood as compensatory epistemic behavior aimed at reducing uncertainty about the perceived loss of interoceptive control.
- **Depends on:** C5, A7
- **Section:** Footnote 4

### I8 — Meditation Adverse Effects Depend on Baseline Threat Orientation
Meditation is most beneficial as exposure therapy for high-avoidance personality types; for low-avoidance types, facilitating attentional bias toward threat can produce or exacerbate anxiety and dissociative disorders.
- **Depends on:** C15, C6
- **Section:** Conclusion

## Key Advancement Over Prior Work

The paper's core contribution relative to Seth et al. (2012) and Gerrans (2019) is threefold:

1. **Valence is constitutive, not epiphenomenal:** Affect is not merely interoceptive perceptual inference but active inference about allostatic control, inherently carrying valence (C4).

2. **Unified framework for dysphoric and euphoric selflessness:** By grounding the self in allostatic control, the paper explains why depersonalization is inherently negative while meditative selflessness is positive -- they are opposite perturbations of the same underlying variable (C14).

3. **Precision on precision:** The paper introduces a meta-level of precision control -- affect sets precision on expected free energy, and meditation grants endogenous control over this process (C3, C9, I5).
