# Claims Extraction: "A Beautiful Loop" (Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria, 2025)

## Logical Structure

---

### Axioms (Imported from prior work)

**A1. Free Energy Principle (FEP)**
- *Statement:* Living systems persist by minimizing variational free energy (equivalently, long-term surprise) over their sensory states.
- *Depends on:* None (foundational axiom from Friston 2010)
- *Section:* Background: Active Inference

**A2. Perceptual inference as belief updating**
- *Statement:* Brains perform approximate Bayesian inference by updating internal beliefs (probability distributions over hidden states) to minimize prediction error against sensory input.
- *Depends on:* A1
- *Section:* Background: Active Inference

**A3. Active inference as action**
- *Statement:* Agents also minimize free energy by acting on the world to make sensory input conform to predictions (expected states).
- *Depends on:* A1
- *Section:* Background: Active Inference

**A4. Hierarchical generative models**
- *Statement:* The brain implements inference through hierarchical generative models: higher levels generate predictions about lower levels; lower levels return prediction errors; precision weighting modulates influence.
- *Depends on:* A2
- *Section:* Background: Active Inference

**A5. Precision weighting**
- *Statement:* The relative influence of prediction errors (bottom-up) vs. priors (top-down) is governed by precision parameters that are themselves inferred.
- *Depends on:* A4
- *Section:* Background: Active Inference

**A6. The binding problem exists**
- *Statement:* There is an unsolved explanatory problem of how distributed neural processes give rise to unified percepts and unified experiential scenes.
- *Depends on:* None (empirical/philosophical background)
- *Section:* Condition 2: Bayesian Binding

---

### Definitions

**D1. Thin agent**
- *Statement:* An agent that minimizes only immediate surprise; it reacts to current states without simulating counterfactual futures. (Example: thermostat.)
- *Depends on:* A1
- *Section:* Background: Agent Depth

**D2. Thick agent**
- *Statement:* An agent that minimizes expected future surprise by navigating counterfactual landscapes -- it simulates "what if" scenarios over an extended temporal horizon. Requires modeling oneself as an agent with multiple possible actions.
- *Depends on:* A1, D1
- *Section:* Background: Agent Depth

**D3. Reality Model (Epistemic Field)**
- *Statement:* A unified, coherent generative model that determines the space of what can be known, interrogated, and acted upon -- the "canvas" of possible experience, bridging inner and outer space.
- *Depends on:* A4
- *Section:* Condition 1

**D4. Bayesian Binding**
- *Statement:* A precision-weighted inferential competition mechanism by which only hypotheses achieving both local coherence (within-level) and global coherence (across-level) win entry into the reality model. Operates at micro-scale (feature binding into percepts) and macro-scale (unifying the experiential field).
- *Depends on:* A5, A6, D3
- *Section:* Condition 2

**D5. Epistemic Depth**
- *Statement:* The degree to which Bayesian beliefs are recurrently shared throughout the hierarchy such that the system's world model contains knowledge of its own existence -- a recursive loop of inference upon inference.
- *Depends on:* A4, D3
- *Section:* Condition 3

**D6. Field-evidencing**
- *Statement:* The property by which the reality model continuously evidences its own knowing, non-locally and structurally, as opposed to representing itself as a discrete content.
- *Depends on:* D5
- *Section:* Condition 3

**D7. Toroidal epistemicity**
- *Statement:* The topological metaphor for the "beautiful loop": beliefs flow through the hierarchy and return to their origin, creating a self-sustaining epistemic cycle with a toroidal structure.
- *Depends on:* D3, D4, D5
- *Section:* The Beautiful Loop

**D8. Hyper-model**
- *Statement:* A meta-level model whose latent states (or parameters) encode and control the overall structure and precision-weighting rules for all layers of inference in the hierarchy.
- *Depends on:* A4, A5
- *Section:* Formal Framework

**D9. Hyper Free Energy**
- *Statement:* A global free energy functional that optimizes the entire hierarchy including meta-parameters, governing precision allocation across levels -- as distinct from local free energy which drives layer-by-layer inference.
- *Depends on:* A1, D8
- *Section:* Formal Framework

**D10. Luminosity**
- *Statement:* The precision of the reflexivity signal (i.e., the strength of the recursive self-evidencing loop). Parameterizes consciousness as a continuum from absent (unconscious) through low (drowsy) to moderate (normal waking) to high (mindful) to maximal (pure consciousness).
- *Depends on:* D5, A5
- *Section:* Formal Framework: Luminosity

---

### Claims (Novel assertions)

**C1. Three conditions are jointly sufficient for consciousness**
- *Statement:* Consciousness arises when and only when three conditions are jointly satisfied: (1) a reality model exists, (2) Bayesian binding governs entry into that model, and (3) epistemic depth creates a recursive loop of self-knowing.
- *Depends on:* D3, D4, D5
- *Section:* Three Conditions for Consciousness / Conclusions

**C2. The reality model is what CAN be conscious**
- *Statement:* The reality model defines the space of possible experiences. It is the necessary substrate -- the "stage" -- upon which conscious contents appear.
- *Depends on:* D3
- *Section:* Condition 1

**C3. Bayesian binding is the selection mechanism for conscious contents**
- *Statement:* What enters consciousness is determined by precision-weighted inferential competition. Bayesian binding solves the classical binding problem within a Bayesian mechanics framework.
- *Depends on:* D4, A6
- *Section:* Condition 2

**C4. Epistemic depth is what makes experience FOR the system**
- *Statement:* Recursive self-evidencing (epistemic depth) is what distinguishes processing that is merely "in" a system from experience that is "for" the system. It is the condition that closes the loop and makes consciousness known.
- *Depends on:* D5, D6
- *Section:* Condition 3

**C5. Epistemic depth is distinct from self-consciousness**
- *Statement:* The recursive knowing described by epistemic depth is NOT higher-order self-representation or reflective self-awareness. It is a structural, non-local, continuous property (field-evidencing), not a discrete represented content.
- *Depends on:* D5, D6
- *Section:* Condition 3

**C6. Consciousness is graded, not binary**
- *Statement:* Consciousness admits of degrees parameterized by luminosity (the precision of the reflexivity signal), ranging continuously from absent to maximal.
- *Depends on:* D10, D5
- *Section:* Formal Framework: Luminosity

**C7. The hyper-model enacts epistemic agency**
- *Statement:* Globally integrated preferences for precision allocation, encoded in the hyper-model, give rise to epistemic agency -- the capacity to direct attention and inquiry -- and the flexibility characteristic of general intelligence.
- *Depends on:* D8, D9
- *Section:* Formal Framework

**C8. Psychedelics increase epistemic depth**
- *Statement:* Psychedelic substances reliably increase epistemic depth by flattening the precision hierarchy (allowing more inferences into the reality model) while simultaneously increasing reflexive signal strength.
- *Depends on:* D5, D10, D4
- *Section:* Applications: Psychedelics

**C9. Pure consciousness events are maximal epistemic depth with minimal content**
- *Statement:* Meditative "pure consciousness" or "minimal phenomenal experience" states arise when epistemic depth (reflexivity) is maximized while representational content of the reality model is minimized.
- *Depends on:* D5, D3, D10
- *Section:* Applications: Meditation

**C10. The three conditions are computationally tractable and testable**
- *Statement:* The three conditions are not merely philosophical stipulations but specify computationally tractable properties that can be tested empirically and implemented in artificial systems.
- *Depends on:* C1, D3, D4, D5
- *Section:* Introduction / Implications for AI

---

### Inferences (Derived consequences)

**I1. Deep sleep is explained by suppressed binding + minimal epistemic depth**
- *Statement:* In deep sleep, the reality model is maintained (condition 1 partially met), but Bayesian binding is suppressed and epistemic depth is minimal, yielding unconsciousness with preserved system integrity.
- *Depends on:* C1, D3, D4, D5
- *Section:* Applications: Sleep and Dreams

**I2. Dreaming is explained by internally generated binding without external constraint**
- *Statement:* In dreaming, the reality model generates content without external sensory constraint, Bayesian binding operates on internal content, and epistemic depth is variable -- yielding immersive experience without external grounding.
- *Depends on:* C1, D3, D4, D5
- *Section:* Applications: Sleep and Dreams

**I3. Flow states involve high binding efficiency with reduced epistemic depth**
- *Statement:* Flow states are characterized by efficient Bayesian binding (optimal performance) combined with reduced epistemic depth (less self-reflection), yielding absorbed pre-reflective engagement.
- *Depends on:* C1, D4, D5, D10
- *Section:* Applications: Flow States

**I4. Current AI systems lack consciousness because they lack epistemic depth**
- *Statement:* Existing AI systems may have reality models (domain representations) and binding-like mechanisms (attention), but they lack epistemic depth -- recursive self-knowing -- and are therefore not conscious under this framework.
- *Depends on:* C1, D5
- *Section:* Implications for AI

**I5. Building the three conditions into an artificial system would (if the theory is correct) produce artificial consciousness**
- *Statement:* If the three conditions are genuinely sufficient, then an artificial system implementing a genuine reality model, Bayesian binding, and epistemic depth would be conscious. This is an empirical question.
- *Depends on:* C1, C10
- *Section:* Implications for AI

**I6. GWT is subsumed as a special case (competition for global workspace)**
- *Statement:* Global Workspace Theory's emphasis on competition for access to a global workspace is captured by the Bayesian binding condition, but the Beautiful Loop framework adds Bayesian grounding and the reflexivity requirement.
- *Depends on:* C1, D4, D5
- *Section:* Relation to Other Theories

**I7. HOT is partially captured but corrected**
- *Statement:* Higher-Order Theories' emphasis on meta-representation is partially captured by epistemic depth, but the Beautiful Loop framework replaces discrete higher-order states with a continuous, distributed architectural property.
- *Depends on:* C4, C5, D5
- *Section:* Relation to Other Theories

**I8. General intelligence requires the capacity to restructure inference itself**
- *Statement:* General and flexible intelligence emerges from hyper-model optimization -- the ability to modify the structure of inference, not just perform inference within a fixed structure.
- *Depends on:* C7, D8, D9
- *Section:* Implications for AI

## What Is Genuinely New

### Core novel contribution

The paper's genuine novelty reduces to **one architectural claim and one key distinction**:

**1. C1 + C4: The epistemic depth condition for consciousness.**
The truly new move is asserting that a third condition -- recursive self-evidencing (epistemic depth) -- is required beyond world-modeling and binding. This is what allegedly separates processing that is "in" a system from experience that is "for" a system. Neither the free energy principle, hierarchical generative models, precision weighting, nor even the idea of binding-as-inference are new. The novel claim is that when the inferential hierarchy loops back on itself with sufficient depth, something qualitatively different emerges: consciousness.

**2. C5: Field-evidencing vs. self-consciousness.**
The distinction between epistemic depth (structural, non-local, continuous self-knowing) and self-consciousness (discrete, represented, reflective) is a genuinely new conceptual move. It attempts to thread the needle between first-order theories (consciousness doesn't require self-knowledge) and higher-order theories (consciousness requires meta-representation) by proposing a third option: structural self-evidencing that is neither first-order processing nor higher-order representation.

### Everything else is:

**Imported framework:**
- The Free Energy Principle (A1-A3)
- Hierarchical generative models and precision weighting (A4-A5)
- The binding problem as a known challenge (A6)
- Predictive processing as a theory of brain function

**Definitional/terminological work:**
- "Reality model," "Bayesian binding," "epistemic depth," "luminosity," "toroidal epistemicity," "hyper-model," "field-evidencing" -- these are new *labels* but largely re-describe existing concepts (generative model, precision-weighted inference, meta-level parameters) with consciousness-theoretic framing
- The thin/thick agent distinction repackages existing ideas about temporal depth in planning

**Derived applications (not independently novel):**
- The explanations of sleep, dreaming, flow, meditation, and psychedelics (I1-I3, C8-C9) follow straightforwardly once you accept C1; they are demonstrations of the framework's scope, not independent contributions
- The AI implications (I4-I5, I8) are conditional on C1 being true
- The theory comparisons (I6-I7) are positioning, not novel claims

### The irreducible kernel

Strip away all framing, and the paper says: **"Take the free energy principle's hierarchical inference seriously. When inference becomes recursive -- when the model's beliefs include beliefs about its own believing -- consciousness arises. The degree of this recursion (luminosity) parameterizes the degree of consciousness. This recursion is structural and continuous, not a discrete higher-order representation."**

That is the paper's genuine contribution. Everything else is either imported machinery or derived consequence.
