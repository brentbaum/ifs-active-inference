# Logical Structure of Chamberlin 2022

**Paper:** Chamberlin, D. E. (2022). "The Active Inference Model of Coherence Therapy." *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 16, 955558.

This document extracts the minimum logical structure of the paper: what is taken as given, what is newly defined, what is novel claimed, and what follows from the combination.

---

## Axioms (Imported from Prior Work)

### A1: Free Energy Principle
**Statement:** Living systems must minimize surprise (free energy) by maintaining a generative model of their environment and minimizing prediction error.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Friston & Stephan 2007; Friston 2009, 2010
**Section:** Introduction to AIMCT / The Bayesian Brain

### A2: Bayesian Brain Hypothesis
**Statement:** The brain performs approximate Bayesian inference, combining prior beliefs with sensory likelihoods to produce posterior beliefs about hidden causes of sensations.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Doya et al. 2006; Parr et al. 2018
**Section:** The Bayesian Brain

### A3: Active Inference
**Statement:** Embodied agents reduce free energy not only by updating beliefs (perceptual inference) but by acting on the world to generate expected observations. Action selection proceeds by evaluating policies according to expected free energy.
**Depends on:** A1, A2
**Source:** Friston et al. 2012; Parr et al. 2022
**Section:** The Active Inference of embodied, embedded agents

### A4: Complete Class Theorem / Bayes Optimal Pathology
**Statement:** For any observed behavior (including pathological behavior), there exists a prior belief under which that behavior is Bayes-optimal. Pathological behavior results from intact inference operating on suboptimal priors, not from broken inference.
**Depends on:** A2
**Source:** Wald 1947; Schwartenbeck et al. 2015; Parr et al. 2018
**Section:** Bayes Optimal Pathology

### A5: Representational Redescription Model
**Statement:** Cognitive development proceeds through phases: implicit procedural knowledge is progressively re-represented at higher levels, becoming explicit and accessible to other cognitive processes. Implicit knowledge is "modular" -- available to the procedure but not to the system as a whole.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Karmiloff-Smith 1979, 1986, 1994; Clark & Karmiloff-Smith 1993
**Section:** Representational redescription begins with implicit representations

### A6: Coherence Therapy Clinical Framework
**Statement:** Coherence Therapy (CT) is an empirically derived experiential psychotherapy based on Psychological Constructivism. It posits that: (a) symptoms are coherent products of implicit models ("emotional truths" / schemas), (b) rendering these implicit models explicit is the central therapeutic task, (c) the process has four phases: Discovery, Integration, Juxtaposition, Verification.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Ecker & Hulley 1996; Ecker et al. 2012; Toomey & Ecker 2007
**Section:** Introduction to Coherence Therapy

### A7: Memory Suppression as Affect Regulation
**Statement:** The brain actively suppresses retrieval of painful memories via right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex inhibiting hippocampal activity. This serves affect regulation but renders the suppressed information unavailable for further processing.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Anderson et al. 2004; Benoit et al. 2014; Depue et al. 2015; Gagnepain et al. 2017
**Section:** Memory suppression regulates affect

### A8: Structure Learning in Active Inference
**Statement:** Structure learning refers to learning the repertoire of models or narratives (not just parameters within a fixed model). It emerges under generative models with spare capacity.
**Depends on:** A3
**Source:** Holmes & Nolte 2019; Smith et al. 2020
**Section:** Discovery effects a representational redescription

### A9: SOMA (Self-Organizing Meta-representational Account)
**Statement:** Consciousness emerges from the brain creating a model of its own activity. Initially non-conscious procedures become objects of representation when their consequences become problematic, producing phenomenal consciousness, agency, and control.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Cleeremans et al. 2020
**Section:** Learning to be conscious

### A10: Stress Impairs Prefrontal Function
**Statement:** Under extreme stress, high norepinephrine compromises frontal lobe function including evaluation of context and planning. This biases action selection toward rapid, low-complexity policies (exploitation over exploration).
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Arnsten et al. 2015; Linson et al. 2018, 2020
**Section:** Active inference and stress

### A11: Epistemic Motivation in Active Inference
**Statement:** Inference is suffused with motivation. The choice of policies for epistemic foraging is biased by motivational states -- sometimes agents seek knowledge, sometimes they pursue ignorance as a desired state.
**Depends on:** A3
**Source:** Kruglanski et al. 2020; Pezzulo et al. 2018
**Section:** Motivation drives the approach or avoidance of knowing

### A12: Global Neuronal Workspace Hypothesis
**Statement:** Consciousness serves to break modularity of non-conscious processing and broadcast information globally across a brain-wide network, making it available for report, working memory, and deliberation.
**Depends on:** --
**Source:** Panagiotaropoulos et al. 2020
**Section:** The emergence of a self with agency

## Definitions

### D1: Implicit Schema (Symptom-Necessitating Schema)
**Statement:** An implicit schema is a construct consisting of: (a) an emotional wound (affective trace of original stressful experience), (b) a presupposition (unexamined assumption about how the world works), and (c) a protective action (behavioral policy adopted to avoid re-experiencing the wound). It operates automatically, outside conscious awareness, and generates the presenting symptom.
**Depends on:** A6
**Section:** CT Clinical Framework / Successful Discovery yields explicit schema (p11)

### D2: Modularity (of Implicit Knowledge)
**Statement:** An implicit schema is "modular" in the sense that it is knowledge *in* the system but not knowledge *to* the system. The knowledge embedded in the procedure is not available to other cognitive processors or to the system as a whole. It cannot be verbally reported, deliberately controlled, or revised.
**Depends on:** A5
**Section:** Representational redescription begins with implicit representations (p6)

### D3: Context-Blind Policy
**Statement:** A policy formed under extreme stress that requires minimal exploration and has minimal parameters, specifically no consideration of context. It fires the same protective action regardless of whether the current situation warrants it.
**Depends on:** D1, A10
**Section:** Active inference and stress (p12)

### D4: Context Embedding
**Statement:** The process by which a formerly context-blind schema becomes context-sensitive: the agent learns a model that contains an appreciation of the schema's former utility and current irrelevance. The schema is recognized as necessary in some contexts but not others.
**Depends on:** D3
**Section:** Resolution mechanism (p14)

### D5: Dyadic Active Inference
**Statement:** The therapeutic process modeled as coordinated active inference between two agents (therapist and client), where the therapist inverts the client's generative model to infer the client's likely suboptimal priors, and designs experiments to test these hypotheses.
**Depends on:** A3, A6
**Section:** Consilience of CT and Bayes Optimal Pathology / What does AICT look like (p5, p8-9)

### D6: Discovery (in AIMCT terms)
**Statement:** The phase of Coherence Therapy recast as a process of active inference and representational redescription. The therapist and client jointly perform active inference to identify the symptom-necessitating schema, progressively re-representing it at higher levels until it becomes explicit, consciously accessible, context-sensitive, and available for deliberation.
**Depends on:** D5, A5, A8
**Section:** Discovery as essential activity / Discovery effects representational redescription (p3-5)

## Claims (Novel Assertions)

### C1: CT and Bayes Optimal Pathology Are Consilient
**Statement:** Coherence Therapy's assumption that symptoms are always coherent (logically necessary given the underlying schema) is formally equivalent to the Bayes Optimal Pathology framework, where pathological behavior is Bayes-optimal under suboptimal prior beliefs.
**Depends on:** A4, A6
**Section:** Consilience of Coherence Therapy and Bayes Optimal Pathology (p4)

### C2: Discovery Is the Primary Therapeutic Mechanism
**Statement:** Successful Discovery of the precise symptom-necessitating schema results in immediate and enduring cessation of the symptom in more than half of clients. Discovery is the only necessary and often sufficient phase of Coherence Therapy.
**Depends on:** A6 (clinical observation from Ecker 2018b; Ecker & Bridges 2020)
**Section:** Discovery as the essential activity of Coherence Therapy (p3)

### C3: Discovery Is Active Inference, Not Memory Retrieval
**Statement:** The AIMCT diverges from prior CT accounts by viewing identification of the symptom-necessitating schema not as "retrieval" of a pre-existing memory, but as the product of coordinated active inference that always acts to re-represent the target schema at a higher hierarchical level. Discovery is knowledge *construction*, not knowledge *recovery*.
**Depends on:** D5, D6, A3, A8
**Section:** Discovery effects a representational redescription (p4-5)

### C4: Discovery Is Structure Learning
**Statement:** In active inference terms, the Discovery phase of CT constitutes structure learning (creating new representations / expanding the model's repertoire), not parametric learning (updating existing belief parameters within a fixed model structure). This fundamentally distinguishes CT from CBT exposure therapy.
**Depends on:** C3, A8
**Section:** Discovery effects a representational redescription (p5)

### C5: Memory Suppression Blocks Representational Redescription
**Statement:** Suppression of episodic memory retrieval renders the schema-related information unavailable for representational redescription and further inference. This is the mechanism by which implicit schemas remain modular and resist change despite contradictory evidence.
**Depends on:** A7, D2
**Section:** Memory suppression interferes with representational redescription (p10)

### C6: Stress Produces Context-Free Schemas
**Statement:** Under extreme stress, the agent selects a protective policy with minimal parameters (no consideration of context). This policy persists as an implicit schema that is context-blind -- it lacks the episodic/contextual detail that would allow it to be context-sensitive. The schema fires in all situations because it was formed without encoding which situations warrant it.
**Depends on:** A10, D3
**Section:** Active inference and stress (p12)

### C7: Resolution Is Context Embedding, Not Erasure
**Statement:** Therapeutic resolution does not involve "unlearning" or "erasing" the schema. Rather, the agent learns a more complex model of itself that contains an appreciation of the schema's former utility and current irrelevance. The schema becomes context-sensitive: recognized as necessary in some contexts but not the present one.
**Depends on:** D4, C2
**Section:** Clinical case vignette / Resolution (p13-14)

### C8: Modularity-Breaking Is the Causal Mechanism of Discovery
**Statement:** The essential mechanism of Discovery is breaking the modularity of the implicit schema. When the schema becomes explicit (non-modular), it is integrated into broader cognitive processing, becomes context-sensitive, and the agent can recognize that the current context does not warrant the protective policy. This modularity-breaking alone can produce symptom resolution without any further belief updating.
**Depends on:** D2, D6, C2, C7, A12
**Section:** Synthesis across sections (p3, p6, p7, p14)

### C9: Therapist Precision Drives Discovery
**Statement:** The therapist's high-precision (high-confidence) belief that the symptom is coherent and that an implicit schema exists and can be found provides the motivational scaffold for the Discovery process. This optimistic prior drives epistemic foraging by the therapeutic dyad.
**Depends on:** D5, A11
**Section:** Recruiting motivation to recall / Self-knowledge as inference (p8, p10-11)

### C10: Discovery Resembles Simulated Annealing
**Statement:** The Discovery process is analogous to simulated annealing: it progressively optimizes model evidence by balancing accuracy and complexity, beginning with broad exploration ("high temperature") and gradually narrowing toward the optimal schema representation ("low temperature"). The therapist regulates the "temperature" of this search.
**Depends on:** D6, C9
**Section:** Discovery resembles simulated annealing (p9)

### C11: CT Addresses Both Suppressed and Never-Represented Content
**Statement:** Coherence Therapy handles not only representations that were suppressed (Freud's "repressed" content) but also influences that have never been represented at all ("inchoate forces" / Psychic Figurability). The active inference framework can account for both: rendering suppressed representations explicit, and constructing representations for influences that were never represented.
**Depends on:** C3, A7
**Section:** The challenge of never represented "inchoate forces" (p5)

### C12: CT Diverges from Psychoanalysis via Active (Not Passive) Inference
**Statement:** While CT converges with Psychoanalysis in using observation to identify hidden causes of symptoms, CT diverges fundamentally by replacing passive observation (Perceptual Inference) with active experimentation (Active Inference). The therapist designs experiments to disambiguate hypotheses rather than offering interpretations.
**Depends on:** D5, A3
**Section:** Divergence of Coherence Therapy from Psychoanalysis (p5-6)

### C13: Attention Directs Low-Confidence Memory Search
**Statement:** The therapeutic question "in what way does the symptom make sense?" initiates a goal-directed, low-confidence memory search mediated by dorsal parietal cortex. The therapist's sustained attention captures products of this search (associations, somatic markers) that the client may not recognize as relevant, scaffolding the representational redescription.
**Depends on:** C9, D6
**Section:** Attention drives low confidence memory search (p11)

### C14: Motivation Recruits Memory Against Suppression
**Statement:** In CT, the discomfort of the symptom provides pragmatic motivation that can overcome the motivational drive to suppress painful memories. The suppressed memory is reframed not as a painful experience to be avoided but as a potential epistemic affordance with pragmatic therapeutic utility.
**Depends on:** A7, A11, C9
**Section:** Recruiting motivation to recall a repressed memory (p10)

### C15: The AIMCT Enables Process Theory
**Statement:** The Bayes Optimal Pathology question ("what priors would make this behavior optimal?") combined with active inference yields a process theory: a set of physiologically plausible mechanisms (memory suppression, modularity, representational redescription, reconsolidation) that explain how suboptimal priors form, persist, and can be therapeutically resolved.
**Depends on:** C1, C5, C6, C8
**Section:** Toward a process theory (p9-10)

## Inferences (Logical Consequences)

### I1: Pathological Behavior Is Informative, Not Dysfunctional
**Statement:** If symptoms are Bayes-optimal under suboptimal priors (A4, C1), then the presenting symptom is the primary data for identifying the hidden schema. The symptom reveals the prior rather than being an error to be corrected.
**Depends on:** A4, C1
**Section:** Consilience of CT and Bayes Optimal Pathology (p4)

### I2: Discovery Can Resolve Symptoms Without Belief Updating
**Statement:** If resolution is context embedding (C7) and the mechanism is modularity-breaking (C8), then rendering the schema explicit in a safe context should produce immediate behavioral change without requiring the agent to update its threat beliefs. The agent simply recognizes that the protective policy is unnecessary *here and now*.
**Depends on:** C7, C8
**Section:** Implied across sections; operationalized in simulation (model_design.md)

### I3: CBT and CT Operate via Different Computational Mechanisms
**Statement:** CBT exposure therapy operates via gradual parametric belief updating (D-matrix learning across many safe exposures). CT operates via structure learning (representational redescription producing a new, context-sensitive model). These produce qualitatively different dynamics: sigmoid-shaped gradual change (CBT) vs. step-function discrete transition (CT).
**Depends on:** C4, C8, A8
**Section:** Implied by contrast with Smith 2021; operationalized in simulation

### I4: Therapy Requires Dyadic Inference, Not Just Client Change
**Statement:** If Discovery requires coordinated active inference (D5) and the therapist's precision drives the search (C9), then the therapeutic process cannot be reduced to client-side belief updating alone. The therapist's generative model of the client is a necessary computational component.
**Depends on:** D5, C9
**Section:** Self-knowledge as inference / What does AICT look like (p8)

### I5: Stress-Formed Schemas Are Resistant to Evidence
**Statement:** If stress produces context-free schemas (C6) and memory suppression blocks redescription (C5), then mere exposure to contradictory evidence (safe outcomes) will not update the schema. The schema is protected from revision by both its context-blindness (it does not register that the current context is different) and memory suppression (the original formation experience is inaccessible for re-representation).
**Depends on:** C5, C6, D2
**Section:** Active inference and stress / Memory suppression (p10, p12)

### I6: Agency Emerges from Representational Redescription
**Statement:** If implicit schemas operate automatically without conscious access (D2) and representational redescription makes them explicit (D6), then the act of making the schema explicit necessarily produces an experience of agency: the agent recognizes itself as the producer of the symptom and gains the possibility of control.
**Depends on:** D2, D6, A9
**Section:** The emergence of a self with agency (p7)

### I7: Not All Clients Resolve at Discovery
**Statement:** If some schemas require not just context-sensitivity but actual belief updating (when the current context *does* warrant the protective response), then Discovery alone is insufficient. These cases require Juxtaposition (exposure to vivid contradictory evidence in the now-explicit/labile state) to produce reconsolidation and belief change.
**Depends on:** C2, C7, C8
**Section:** Implied by the >50% figure (p3) -- the remaining cases need Juxtaposition

### I8: Suboptimal Priors Can Ground Phenotyping
**Statement:** If symptoms arise from identifiable suboptimal priors (C1, C15), and these priors share common features across individuals (formed early in life, in emotionally charged situations), then the identification and categorization of suboptimal priors can serve as a basis for computational phenotyping in psychiatry.
**Depends on:** C1, C15
**Section:** Conclusion (p14)

## What Is Genuinely New in This Paper

Stripping away the imported frameworks, the paper's novel contribution reduces to:

1. **The identification C1:** CT's clinical axiom ("symptoms are coherent") maps onto Bayes Optimal Pathology formally.

2. **The mechanism claim C8:** The *specific* mechanism of Discovery is modularity-breaking (not belief updating, not insight-as-interpretation, not memory retrieval). Making the schema context-sensitive is often sufficient for resolution.

3. **The distinction C4:** CT constitutes structure learning; CBT constitutes parameter learning. These are computationally distinct.

4. **The reframing C3:** Discovery is active inference (knowledge construction via coordinated experimentation), not memory retrieval from a different memory system.

5. **The process theory C15:** Connecting memory suppression, stress-induced context-blindness, modularity, and representational redescription into a single mechanistic account of how schemas form, persist, and resolve.

Everything else is either imported axiom, definition, or logical consequence of these five claims.
