The Suite v2 model, and what therapy does to it

A visual guide to the IFS active-inference model: how protective parts form, what changes during therapy, and what breaks when one therapeutic ingredient is removed.

How to read this

IFS describes an inner system of vulnerable parts and protectors. This page asks whether those clinical patterns can arise in one generative model — a model that explains observations by inferring hidden causes. Bayesian inference starts with a prior, the model's belief before new evidence, and produces a posterior, its updated belief afterward. The hidden causes are latent variables: quantities the model cannot observe directly. Keep a dog-phobia example in mind. After a dog bite, barking may support not only “dogs are dangerous” but “I am the one who is unsafe around dogs.” Avoidance prevents new bites and also hides safe encounters that could correct the belief. Therapy may make the old scene bearable to revisit, so a calm dog can be learned as now without erasing what happened then. Suite v2 tests each link. It was built one rung at a time. Each rung added a small set of hidden variables, then passed six checks: meaning, recovery of simulated truths, open tests, selective lesions, checks that earlier results still hold, and a sealed prospective challenge whose details stay hidden until the stage is frozen. A lesion removes one modeled route to test whether only its predicted effect disappears. Formally, the frozen bundle remains a model. Functionally, fast inference proceeds within it rather than repeatedly comparing it with live alternatives; slower learning can still revise it.

This is the reference view. For a step-by-step introduction that builds the same model one mechanism at a time, each mechanism motivated clinically before it appears, see the model build-up page.

This page offers three views of the same construction: the full model; the therapy sequence; and comparator fingerprints showing how therapy fails when one ingredient is removed. Formation and maintenance appear in the part-structure and action-attribution nodes. Redescription appears in the context node. Unburdening appears in the burden-coupling nodes. Protector trust and policy explain whether contact becomes tolerable.

The suite is complete as of 2026-07-29 — every stage frozen, every sealed challenge expressible and run once, and every retained stop still in the ledger below.

One rule shapes everything drawn here. The suite's scientific-state constitution says the model may never write a therapeutic conclusion directly into its state. Every changing scientific quantity must be a posterior over a hidden state, a posterior over a learnable parameter, model evidence — how well a candidate explanation predicts the observations — or a pure readout, a summary computed from those quantities and never written back. Nothing may assign formed, redescribed, reduced, permission, access, unburdened, or any therapeutic-stage label. There is no gate object and no polarization coefficient. 3  What the model believes before anything happens: the prior inventory

Every simulated life begins with the declared starting beliefs below. The model is never handed a mature part, protector mandate, or exile story. Its history must teach it those patterns. Solid-edged entries are choices among discrete structures: for example, “temporary threat” versus “persistent danger” versus “part-like organization.” Dashed entries are uncertain numerical parameters that can be learned gradually. Dotted entries are fixed settings of the evidence machinery. The exact values are preserved so you can see what the model assumed before it saw anything.

5  One slice, one packet: the one-posterior rule in motion

One experience should update only the beliefs it actually bears on. A safe-dog encounter can be evidence about dogs and context; a therapist staying after a protector says “no” can be evidence about the therapist. The model may not quietly use either event to change an unrelated belief. Formally, each observation updates only the posteriors named by its typed likelihood; all others remain bit-for-bit identical. Choose a token and watch where it may go.

The patterns are clinically distinct. Regulation alone helps the system settle, but does not revise identity or burden. Cue exposure changes the practiced cue but does not generalize to another dog. Bypassing protectors produces contact in 100% of worlds, yet trust does not improve, reduction never occurs, and later stress produces the largest rupture. A merely soothing or unreliable partner does not create the same global openness to evidence. Monitoring without broadcast stays local. A premature do-over reaches the story but not stable reduction. Removing the aloneness update (no_registration in code), context learning, or reduction deletes only that target.

What the suite may and may not claim

The completed suite supports one narrow claim: one exact, cumulatively tested construction reproduced the full modeled sequence and produced the published mechanism and challenge profile. It does not show that the therapy works in people, estimate how common these patterns are, prove that IFS is the only useful language, or show that every step is necessary in clinical practice. A simulation result is evidence about this construction, not a verdict on a person.
