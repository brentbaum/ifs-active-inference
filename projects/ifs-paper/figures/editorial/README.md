### Proposed caption for the four-protector network

**Figure X. Protector organization as a relational control system.** Incoming
activation (gray waves) is restricted by protector policies (shielding marks)
before it can fully reactivate an exile. The topology is many-to-many rather
than nested: one protector can restrict access to one vulnerable state, several
protectors can converge on the same exile, and one protector can regulate access
to more than one exile. In the dog example, Protector 2 might prospectively scan
the street, avoid parks, and plan escape routes, while Protector 4 triggers
immediate flight or numbing when a dog appears. The two protectors are allied
because their different policies serve the same expected outcome: preventing
Exile 2—the child who was cornered by the dog and expects helplessness—from
becoming active. Protector 4 also guards Exile 3, illustrating how a policy first
recruited around one vulnerable state can later control access to another, such
as a part carrying the expectation that no one will come to help. Protectors can
also polarize. Protector 1 may insist that all contact with dogs be avoided,
whereas Protector 3 may insist on approaching dogs to prove that the person is
not afraid. Their policies oppose one another, but both are organized around
preventing the return of an intolerable self-state. The diagram therefore
represents the protective system as interacting predictions and policies:
alliances coordinate control, polarization produces conflict, and the effective
gate to each exile is the joint output of the protectors currently active.

### Proposed caption for trust and permission

**Figure X. Schematic trajectories of protector trust and permission across
repeated encounters.** In Panel A, low initial trust is a precise pessimistic
forecast, represented by a narrow band rather than uncertainty. Smooth contacts
produce small updates because the protector can explain them as temporary or
strategic. A refusal tests the policy behind the request; respecting it supplies
a modest increment. Rupture is read as diagnostic and sharply confirms the old
model, whereas repair produces the largest update because it is evidence the old
model cannot readily explain away. With repetition, gains become larger, losses
shallower, and uncertainty narrows around a more favorable forecast. Panel B
shows that permission is not a thresholded copy of trust: it may remain closed
while trust rises, can be revoked immediately after rupture or circumvention,
and may reopen only after trust has substantially recovered. Hope is not shown
because it changes the comparison among available policies rather than the
forecast that contact will end well.

## Shared final prompt

> Create a scientific-educational figure for a theoretical academic paper on a
> pure clean white background. Use a calm minimalist editorial illustration
> style: simple flat lightly hand-drawn forms, thin clean slightly organic
> linework, rounded gentle geometry, abundant negative space, and an
> instructional, contemplative feel. Use a restrained matte palette of soft sage,
> warm terracotta, dusty peach, light taupe, pale gray-green, and faded charcoal.
> Keep contrast soft but preserve structural legibility at journal-page size.
> Typography must be minimal, quiet, and exactly spelled. Avoid titles, captions,
> gradients, shadows, 3D effects, bright colors, corporate infographic styling,
> watermarks, and any text not explicitly requested.
