## The Central Shift: What the Paper Is Actually About

**v6 thought it was about:** The blending/witnessing distinction — formalizing what IFS calls the difference between being a part and being with a part.

**v7 knows it is about:** Parts. What they are formally. Why that formal definition explains both IFS phenomenology and IFS theory of change.

The carry-forward sentence — the one another scholar would borrow — is now:

> *"A part is not a fear memory. It is an identity-level precision bundle in which self-state is the organizing prior — and revision that reaches the root generalizes in ways that revision of threat meaning alone cannot."*

Everything else in the paper is downstream of this.

---

## The Three-Move Structure (Settled v8)

The paper has three distinct theoretical moves:

**Move 1 — Parts as identity-level precision bundles**
Defines the formal object. A part bundles four priors: self-state, world-state, policy, expected outcome. *Self-state is the organizing prior* — not just one element among four, but the root from which the others derive. This is what makes parts identity-level rather than merely stimulus-specific.

Explains:
- Why parts feel like whole worlds (bundle couples all four elements)
- Why activation feels like identity, not belief (precision dominates, no vantage outside)
- Why parts persist (high prior precision + avoidant sampling)
- Why parts form (overwhelm + low control consolidates the bundle)
- Why polarization happens (two bundles assigning danger to each other's policies)

**Move 2 — Self-energy as precision governor**
Explains the theory of change. Self-energy determines the precision balance between active part priors and present-context evidence. That balance determines inferential regime: capture or context-held activation.

Explains:
- Why some activations revise and others repeat
- Why witnessing permits lasting change (context stays online)
- Why dissociation differs from witnessing (context is turned down, not maintained)
- Why exposure differs from witnessing (different inferential regime, not different information)

**Move 3 — Relational prediction error (added v8, 2026-03-26)**
Explains what happens *inside* the witnessing window. At sufficient Self-energy depth, the system can observe its own present-moment self-state. The part's relational expectation (isolation) encounters Self's presence. That identity-level prediction error reaches the organizing prior directly. Move 3 is Move 2 at sufficient depth — not a separate mechanism.

Explains:
- Why the core of IFS change is relational, not informational
- Why witnessing without life-updating can suffice for identity-level revision
- Why exposure doesn't produce the same depth of change (no relational PE)
- Why modality doesn't matter (visual, somatic, inner-voice all work — the relational registration is what counts)
- Why parts detect the difference between Self-energy and caring-part energy

**Together:** Move 1 explains the phenomenology of activation. Move 2 opens the window for change. Move 3 explains what happens inside the window that produces revision. H1 (self-state upstream) is definitionally entailed by Move 1, not a separate empirical hypothesis.

## H1 Is Not a Hypothesis

In v6, H1 (self-state upstream of threat meaning, upstream of policy) was framed as a testable hypothesis compared against H2.

**The reframe:** H1 is *definitionally entailed* by what IFS calls a "part" as distinct from a fear memory. A fear memory is threat-primary (H2). A part is identity-primary (H1) — that is precisely what makes it a part rather than a conditioned fear response.

You don't test H1 against H2 to see which is right. You use the H1/H2 comparison to show that the bundle's identity-upstream structure produces a revision cascade that standard fear-learning models (H2) cannot predict. H1 is the formal content of the IFS clinical claim; H2 is what the standard account implicitly assumes.

**Implication:** The H1/H2 simulation comparison is the paper's strongest empirical move — it shows the bundle definition doing explanatory work that flat fear-learning models cannot do.

## Object Relations Convergence (Added to §4)

The fourfold bundle structure has independent precedent in object relations theory:

| Active inference bundle | Object relations |
|---|---|
| Self-state | Self-image |
| World-state | Object-image |
| Policy | Cause-image |
| Expected outcome | Effect-image |

These two frameworks arrived at the same structure from entirely different directions. Object relations also treats self-image as the root: "Relating to the Part as Presence helps to update the Part's *image of the self*" — not threat meaning first, not policy first. Self-image first.

**Why this matters:** It confirms that the bundle structure is not a modeling choice. It is capturing something real about the structure of agentive inner experience, with convergent support from a completely different tradition.

A comment was added to §4 to preserve this connection: `<!-- KEEP: This mapping to object relations is load-bearing... Do not cut. -->`

## The Blending/Witnessing Symmetry Problem

**v6:** Blending and witnessing were a symmetric pair — two coequal states. This made the paper feel like it was naming two things rather than explaining one mechanism.

**v7:** The framing is now asymmetric.

- **Capture** is the mechanistic term for what happens when a bundle dominates inference.
- **Context-held activation** is the mechanistic term for the favorable regime.
- **Blending** is the clinical name for capture (introduced as such).
- **Witnessing** is the therapeutically cultivated form of context-held activation (introduced as such, not as the simple opposite of blending).

The paper is now about the mechanism (capture vs. context-held), not about labeling two states. Witnessing remains important — it is the clinically named, cultivated form of the goal state — but it is no longer forced into a flat binary with blending.

## Discriminant Validity (Added to §4)

A new paragraph in §4 shows what identity-level precision bundles explain that schemas, latent contexts, and trait priors cannot:

- **Schemas** encode patterns but don't couple identity-state + world-state + policy + outcome as a single activation unit. A schema can update without the system re-entering the identity position that organized it.
- **Latent contexts** select which model is active but are structurally flat — they don't encode that self-state is the root of the bundle.
- **Trait priors** encode stable individual differences but don't explain why activation feels like regression to an earlier self-position rather than recall of a fear memory.

Three things follow from the bundle structure that none of the above can explain:
1. Why parts feel like whole worlds (not isolated beliefs)
2. Why change at identity level generalizes while change at threat level stays local
3. Why activation feels like age regression (re-entering a developmental self-position), not fear recall

This paragraph is the paper's answer to the computational reviewer who asks: "Isn't this just a relabeling?"

## Empirical Targets (Added to §14.4)

Two concrete falsifiable predictions now lead §14.4:

**Revision order prediction:** Under IFS-informed contact (witnessing), self-state should cross its revision threshold before threat meaning, which should cross before policy/avoidance. Under standard exposure, all three should move more uniformly without the predicted cascade. Requires purpose-built measures that separate:
- Self-state: "When I encounter X, I feel like my younger/smaller self"
- Threat meaning: "X is genuinely dangerous to me now"
- Policy: behavioral approach/avoidance index

**Generalization gradient prediction:** IFS-like change should generalize to novel feared stimuli more than exposure-based change. Test: after treatment targeting one fear, measure transfer to a structurally similar untrained fear. The model predicts IFS transfer exceeds exposure transfer because IFS changes the identity-level prior (the root), not just the specific threat association.

These are the paper's clearest targets for empirical collaborators and should be the first thing mentioned in any "future directions" conversation.

## Argumentative Spine (Agreed — Draft B)

1. The same part activation can either take over a person's entire field or be held as something they can relate to — IFS treats that difference as the fulcrum of therapeutic change.
2. We formalize parts as identity-level precision bundles, where self-state is the root: *who I am here* determines *what this means* and *what I must do*.
3. Self-energy determines whether the active bundle captures inference or whether present-context evidence stays online alongside it.
4. The self-state-upstream structure predicts a revision cascade that exposure alone cannot produce: change at identity level generalizes; change at threat level stays local.
5. Simulations support the cascade order and distinguish witnessing, exposure, and dissociation by their revision profiles.

Note: Step 2 still needs workshopping. Vocabulary candidates: "identifying," "self-belief." The concept is clear; the sentence isn't yet earning it.

The single biggest theoretical addition. Discovered through a chain:

5. Two evidence channels: relational (primary, always load-bearing) and informational (secondary, not always necessary). Witnessing exists to serve the relational update.

## The Self Decomposition (Regime + Presence)

Dick Schwartz says Self is "a particle and a wave." We don't use that metaphor but the formal content is:
- Self-energy (regime): the precision governor. Determines capture vs context-held. The field, the container.
- Self's present-moment self-state (presence): what emerges when the regime is in place. What parts register. What generates relational PE.

The paper's §6.1 now says both: Self is a regime of uncaptured inference, AND when that regime obtains, the system's present-moment self-state becomes available as a differentiated presence that parts can register.

## Simulation V2: Three-Move Model

- 3 hidden factors (self-state, threat meaning, expected outcome). Context environmental. Policy derived.
- 5 observation channels. Channel 5 = "witnessed self-state" — precision gated by inverse capture.
- The cascade diagonal in the heatmap IS the paper's argument.
- Free-choice probe differentiates three conditions: Exposure=avoid, Informational=inspect, Relational Depth=stay.
- H1 vs H2: cascade present under H1, absent under H2.
- Self-energy sweep shows sigmoid threshold at E_t ≈ 0.60-0.65.

## Polarization V2: Two Parts, One System

Part A (exile: avoid dog) vs Part B (social manager: approach human). Mixture-of-experts.
- Low E_t: oscillation or one-part capture.
- Medium E_t: awkward compromise.

- Clinical insight confirmed: you don't resolve a parts war by choosing one side.

## 10 Figure Design Patterns (from Chamberlin 2022 analysis)

1. **Step-function / phase transition** — make the cascade look like something SHIFTED, not gradual drift
2. **Identical axes across condition panels** — direct visual comparison without mental rescaling
3. **Color hierarchy** — consistent colors for conditions; muted for controls, saturated for key result
4. **Confidence bands over error bars** — mean + shaded region for stochastic runs
5. **Small multiples** — one panel per bundle element, shared time axis
6. **Annotated intervention points** — vertical dashed line at phase transitions, labeled
7. **Effect size prominence** — make the key result visually dominant; don't equalize conditions
8. **Mechanism inset** — small causal diagram accompanying the data figure
9. **First-passage markers** — dots/ticks showing when each element crosses threshold
10. **Vertical divider between phases** — forced contact | free choice, clearly marked

Source: Chamberlin 2022 Coherence Therapy figures (ct_schematic, ct_mechanism_comparison, ct_before_after, ct_trajectories, discovery_mechanism). Full analysis in `figure-inspiration.md`.
