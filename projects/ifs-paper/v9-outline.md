## §1. Introduction (~600w)

**Opens with:** layered unmet-need/protector stack (not dog fear).

> A child needs comfort and protests when it is unavailable. That protest is adaptive at first. Later it becomes humiliating, punished, or ignored. A new strategy forms to inhibit protest. Later still, even the wish to ask directly may become shameful, and another protector forms around that. What persists is not just fear, but a layered system in which each strategy is organized around the anticipated cost of allowing the layer beneath it to come forward.

Then: "Sometimes *I am afraid.* Sometimes *a part of me is afraid.*" — salvaged from v8.

**Core claim stated early:**

> We model IFS as a relational system in which parts are identity-level bundles whose world-models naturally include other parts; protectors are bundles whose policies include access-control over lower-layer states; Self-energy governs whether a broader self-anchor ("Presence") can hold those interactions without capture; and witnessing works by relaxing protective predictions enough for burdened self-states to revise.

**What the paper does not claim:**
- Not a full IFS ontology (formalizes burdened/extreme roles)
- Not an argument that IFS replaces other therapies
- Not a simulation of the whole IFS arc

**Roadmap:** brief (3-4 sentences), §2-8.

## §2. IFS and the Problem to Be Formalized (~400w)

Very short. No extended narration. No dog.

- Parts: exiles carry terror, shame, helplessness, unmet need
- Protectors: managers (prospective) and firefighters (reactive) — full parts with roles, fears, strategies
- Self: not another part; a regime + a broader self-anchor (Presence)
- The diagnostic question: "How do you feel toward this part?" — measures relationship to activation
- The unsolved problem: IFS has lacked a formal account of what determines whether contact leads to revision or repetition

## §3. Formalization: Parts in Hierarchical Relation (~800w)

### The exile bundle
- Self-state (including developmental time-position: "I am six," "I am in the past")
- World-state (what kind of situation this is)
- Policy (what I must do)
- Expected outcome (what will happen)
- Salvage: discriminant validity paragraph (schemas, contexts, traits can't predict these three things)
- Salvage: carry-forward sentence (adapted)

### The protector bundle
- Role/self-position ("I'm the one who keeps us safe/hidden/good")
- Target-part model ("if that crying/protesting part gets through, then...")
- Policy priors (suppress, redirect, perfect, numb, charm, attack...)
- Feared consequences (humiliation, abandonment, flooding, loss of control)
- **Gating is a function of protector policies, not what protectors are**

### Three Levels of Relation (explicit subsection)
1. **Protector to exile:** organized around predicted cost of exile's need/pain becoming live
2. **Protector to protector:** later protectors wrap earlier ones when those strategies become costly/shaming
3. **Self to the stack:** witnessing = protector learns contact with exile no longer implies catastrophe

### Brief formation (1-2 paragraphs)
- Overwhelm + low control + insufficient co-regulation → bundle crystallization
- Not all fear becomes a part; chronic neglect can be part-forming without catastrophe

### Brief persistence (1 paragraph)
- Self-sealing loop: high precision + underweighted context + avoidant sampling
- Functional isolation (channels available but underweighted)

## §4. Layered Protection and Relational Gating (~1000w)

THE new section. Four moves:

### Move 1: Protector defined relationally
A protector is a full part whose policies include gating access to lower-layer states, learned because contact with those states was predicted to be costly (flooding, shame, destabilization, social punishment).

### Move 2: Layered protection
New protectors form when prior protective strategies themselves become dangerous, shameful, or ineffective. The system becomes a stack of gates around the original wound.

**Developmental story:** wound → first strategy → strategy becomes punished/humiliating → second protector → wish to ask becomes shameful → third protector → stack

### Move 3: "Aging up"
Later protective layers encode developmentally later self-states and more socially elaborated policies. The burdened layer below remains developmentally frozen. This is not literal aging of the exile — it is layering of increasingly sophisticated strategies around a frozen core.

### Move 4: Witnessing redefined
Safe descent through the gate hierarchy under conditions where protectors can update their predictions about what contact with lower layers will cost.

IFS spends so much time getting permission from protectors first because the protector is a gatekeeper estimating the expected cost of access. The therapeutic sequence is not "open the gate" but "build trust, validate the protector's positive intent, address its fears, and offer hope for a new role."

### Other parts in the world-model
A part's world-state beliefs naturally include other parts — they are part of the environment. This means:
- Alliances = convergent world-models + compatible policies
- Polarizations = incompatible policies under limited control bandwidth
- These emerge from the relational architecture without a separate mechanism

**One diagram here:** parts with world-model arrows, protector-to-exile gating, protector-to-protector layering, Self relating to the stack.

## §6. Relational Prediction Error (~800w)

### The exile form (salvage from v8 §9)
- Part expects isolation → encounters Self's presence → identity-level mismatch → reaches organizing prior
- "The part expected isolation. It encountered presence."
- Salvage: Anderson quote, "She sees me now" (Schwartz), modality independence + Mosaic Mind quote

### The protector form (NEW — the key upgrade)
- Protector expects contact with exile will overwhelm, shame, destabilize, or expose the system
- Instead encounters Self remaining present, regulated, and non-collapsed
- Gate cost estimate updates → protector relaxes → access becomes possible
- **Clinical ordering: protectors usually change before exiles do**

### Self witnesses, does not meet needs
- What revises is the prediction that contact with the need is intolerable
- Not that Self internally satisfies the need
- The need becomes contactable and expressible without triggering old protective predictions

### Witnessing = window
- Makes later processes possible (retrieval, unburdening, reintegration)
- These are acknowledged but only minimally represented

### Unburdening
- Durable collapse of burdened attractor basin:
  - Gate no longer needs to stay closed
  - Exile self-state revised (unmet-alone → held-capable)
  - Adaptive policy available without protector takeover
- "Unburdening is the durable collapse of a burdened attractor basin rather than a distinct ontological event"
- Role transformation: former protector takes on a new functional role

### Clinical probe
- "Say 'I am presence' — if parts object, the objecting protector is the current gatekeeper"
- In theory section as diagnostic of gate opposition, not in simulation

### Reconsolidation bridge (brief, softened)
- Model is reconsolidation-compatible, not claiming to have demonstrated reconsolidation

## §7. Simulation Evidence (~1200w)

Written around real results from v4 simulation (Session 6-7).

### Architecture (brief)
- Three hidden factors: gate state, exile self-state, meaning/cost of contact
- Four observation channels: need cue, interoceptive, external response, Presence
- Four policies: suppress, protest, stay-with, direct ask
- Gate is hidden factor inferred from observations (not flipped by Presence)
- Simulation-theory gap stated: "represents the effective gate state — net output of protector policies — as a single hidden factor, while theory allows richer protector bundles"

### Three conditions
1. **Full witnessing:** Presence + corrective external → gate opens first → self-state → meaning → direct ask
2. **Relational-only:** Presence, external only neutral → gate opens, self moves, meaning less
3. **Informational-only:** corrective external, no Presence → meaning moves, gate/self barely move

### Gate-ablation
- Block Presence → Gate channel → advantage disappears
- Isolates the relational-gating mechanism

### Transfer probe
- Train on cue A, probe on cue B (shared gate + self-state, cue-specific meaning)
- Witnessing transfers; informational-only does not

## §8. Discussion (~800w)

### What the model explains
- Layered protection and gate traversal
- Why IFS spends time on protector permission
- Why witnessing generalizes (shared gate + self-state)
- Unburdening as regime change

### What remains unmodeled
- Stacked multiple protector layers (theory has it, simulation collapses to one gate)
- Full cross-part dynamics (alliances, polarization beyond theory section)
- Therapist as second agent
- Retrieval, imaginal unburdening, role reintegration

### Division-of-labor caution
- Theory formalizes hierarchical relational gating
- Simulation tests one consequence: witnessing-regime identity-level revision outperforms threat-level updating

### Stated limitations
- Witnessing = window, not whole arc
- Model formalizes burdened/extreme roles, not full IFS ontology
- Self-like parts unsolved
- Foil narrowed: not strawmanning AEDP/EFT/Coherence Therapy
- Some protectors operate on accurate current contingencies — not pathologizing realistic protection

### Empirical predictions
- Gate relaxation ordering (protectors first, then exiles)
- Generalization gradient (identity-level transfer > threat-level)
- Relational > informational channel

### What a stacked-gating simulation would need
- Brief pointer to future work
