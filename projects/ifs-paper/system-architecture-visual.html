Figure 1. Generative Model Architecture -- IFS Active Inference Simulation (v2)

Three hidden factors, five observation channels, self-energy modulated precision, depth-gated self-observation

Hidden Factors (top row) Three binary latent states form the generative model's core. Self-state encodes both identity ("I am helpless") and relational expectation ("I am alone"). Under H1, they are causally ordered: self-state drives threat meaning, which drives expected outcome. Under H2 (control), threat is upstream of self-state -- the cascade disappears.
Self-Energy Modulation A single scalar Et continuously rebalances two precision streams. At low Et (exposure), part precision dominates and burdened priors resist revision. At high Et (relational depth), context precision grows, capture drops, and the system can update.
Observation Channels 1-4 Each channel maps from specific hidden factors through its A-matrix. Ch 1 (external cue) depends on threat meaning. Ch 2 (interoception) depends on both self-state and threat meaning. Ch 3 (action outcome) depends on expected outcome plus environment. Ch 4 (informational context) depends on self-state, threat, and environment.
Channel 5: Witnessed Self-State The key innovation. This channel has near-perfect fidelity (0.995) mapping self-state to observation, but its precision is gated by inverse capture. Only when capture is sufficiently low AND context precision exceeds an absolute floor does the system "see" its own present-moment self-state. This is where Move 3 lives -- witnessing emerges from sufficient decapture, not from a separate mechanism.
Two-Stage Update Each timestep runs inference in two stages: (1) update beliefs from channels 1-4, then (2) compute the capture index from those updated beliefs and open channel 5 accordingly. This prevents circularity where channel 5 affects the capture it depends on.
Policy Selection via EFE Expected Free Energy integrates current beliefs and expected outcome to select among three policies: avoid, inspect, stay. The expected outcome prior biases EFE -- when the bundle believes "avoidance saves," EFE favors avoidance. Policy is a readout of the cascade, not a hidden factor.
Environmental Context The environment (safe/dangerous) generates observations on channels 1, 3, and 4, but is NOT inferred as a hidden factor. This is a design choice: the scenario holds context fixed (safe park with friendly dog) while varying only self-energy across conditions. The real-danger control uses a dangerous context to test specificity.
D Priors: Burdened Initialization All three factors start with burdened priors reflecting the part's learned beliefs: self-state 90/10 helpless, threat meaning 80/20 dangerous, expected outcome 75/25 avoidance. These are the priors that must be revised for the cascade to occur. The decreasing strength (90 > 80 > 75) reflects the causal chain -- upstream elements are more strongly held.

Figure 1. System architecture of the IFS active inference simulation (v2). The generative model consists of three binary hidden factors connected in a causal chain (H1 architecture), five observation channels with factor-specific likelihood mappings, and a self-energy parameter that continuously modulates the precision balance between part-level and context-level evidence streams. Channel 5 (witnessed self-state) is precision-gated by inverse capture, implementing the paper's central claim that witnessing is decapture at sufficient relational depth, not a separate mechanism. Policy selection via Expected Free Energy translates revised beliefs into behavioral change. See simulation-v2-spec.md for full parameterization.
