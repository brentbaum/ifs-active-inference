## 1. Introduction

Sometimes I am afraid. Sometimes a part of me is afraid. The activation may be identical — the same racing heart, the same narrowing of the world — but the two sentences describe different relationships to it, and the difference between them is — this paper's claim — the decisive variable in therapeutic change.

Consider someone who was cornered by a dog as a child and meets one, decades later, on a sidewalk. What happens next is poorly described as the retrieval of a fear memory — but not, either, as a felt transformation. The appraisals of a frightened child retake inference — *this dog is dangerous, I can't handle this, I have to get away* — and nothing marks them as a child's. The person does not feel herself become small; she simply feels incapable, in the present tense, with no seam between then and now. Threat appraisal, sense of self, policy, and expected catastrophe operate as one package, and while it holds it is not one belief among many; it is the reality being reasoned within. If the shift shows itself at all, it is only afterwards: *what came over me?* The signature, then, is not that activation feels like becoming someone else — it is that one has become someone else without anything feeling like it, and that change reaching this level generalizes in ways cue-level change does not. That signature is what this paper sets out to explain.

Internal Family Systems therapy (IFS; Schwartz & Sweezy, 2020) treats this signature as its central datum. In IFS terms the fear belongs to a *part* — a burdened sub-personality carrying the original terror — protected by other parts that manage exposure to it; and the clinical question is not how intense the fear is but who is relating to it: whether the person is *captured* by the part or present with it as *Self*. IFS's own gate for proceeding is a felt quality it calls Self-energy.

This paper gives that clinical structure a computational reading. Our thesis is that the decisive therapeutic variable is the system's relation to activated part-content, that the governing quantity behind this relation is Self-energy, and that both can be stated precisely in active inference terms. Three claims carry the account:

**C1 — Frozen Identity.** A part is an identity-level precision bundle — a self-state prior, world-state priors, policy priors, and expected outcomes that learned together and reactivate together — organized around the self-state prior as its root. A part is *frozen process* — our adaptation of Gendlin's (1964) "frozen wholes" — a bundle whose precision has risen until it functions as fixed architecture rather than as one hypothesis among many.

**C2 — Regime.** Self-energy is epistemic depth: the reflexivity precision of Laukkonen and Chandaria (2024). It sets the precision balance between part priors and present evidence. Under capture the bundle is *transparent* — perceived through, operating as the reality model; with sufficient depth the same bundle is *opacified* — represented as an object the system can relate to (Metzinger, 2003; Limanowski & Friston, 2018). Only an opacified prior can revise.

**C3 — Relational Revision.** If C2 says *when* revision is possible, C3 says *what does the revising*. Two corrections are available in the same encounter: informational — *this dog is not dangerous* — which revises the threat meaning attached to a cue; and relational — *I am not alone with this* — which revises the self-state at the bundle's root. We propose that the relational error is the operative signal in deep therapeutic change, and because self-states are shared across situations while threat meanings are cue-bound, its revision generalizes beyond the treated cue.

IFS's basic taxonomy — *exiles*, which carry the wounds, and *protectors*, which manage the system away from them — falls out before any new machinery is added. Exile activation carries a forecastable cost, and the policies that keep it out of reach crystallize, under conditions the paper will give, into parts of their own. The exile is the system frozen around the wound; the protector is the system frozen around managing it. The protectors-first ordering of IFS practice then follows from the architecture rather than from procedural convention.

Two non-claims bound the account. We do not claim IFS is the only therapy that works, or that exposure-based methods do nothing; different therapies alter different inferential variables, and we will say which one this account targets. And the simulations that implement these claims are existence proofs and prediction generators, not confirmations; the confirmatory tests are the empirical predictions stated at the end.

The account joins a family of active inference treatments of self-transformative practice — meditation and insight (Laukkonen & Slagter, 2021), epistemic depth (Laukkonen & Chandaria, 2024), emptiness realisation (Sandved-Smith et al., 2026), depersonalisation (Deane et al., 2020). Those programs revise priors through attentional and metacognitive operations on the global self-model. The present account targets intermediate, part-level identity priors through an internal *relationship*. Relational prediction error is the move the family does not yet have.

Section 2 states IFS in its own terms. Sections 3–5 develop the architecture: what a part is (§3), how one freezes (§4), and how protection organizes and stratifies around it (§5). Sections 6–8 develop the dynamics: Self and Self-energy (§6), capture and context-held activation (§7), and the relational revision that melts what froze and descends the protective system (§8). Section 9 summarizes the simulations implementing the claims, and Section 10 states the account's empirical predictions and its principal departure from IFS orthodoxy.

## 2. IFS in its own terms

IFS models the mind as a natural multiplicity of subsystems — *parts* — in relationship with a seat of awareness it calls *Self*, which is not itself a part. Parts that carry the pain of overwhelming experience are *exiles*: young in feel, frozen at the time of the wound, held out of awareness because their material floods. Around them organize *protectors*: *managers*, which work proactively so that exiled material is never triggered, and *firefighters*, which react when it surfaces anyway. Self is not another part with better ideas; it is who is relating when no part has taken over, recognized clinically by qualities — curiosity, calm, compassion, clarity — rather than by any content. The felt availability of those qualities under activation is *Self-energy*, and it is the method's go/no-go gate: with it, contact with a part proceeds; without it, the therapist works with whatever part is blocking it first.

In these terms the dog episode reads: an exile carries the original terror and helplessness; a manager scans sidewalks and crosses streets early; when a dog gets close anyway, a firefighter takes over with panic or flight. While the person is *blended* — what we have been calling captured — there is no one home besides the part; the fear speaks in the first person, and IFS calls this *speaking from* the part. With Self-energy present, the same activation can be *spoken for*: "a part of me is terrified." Same fear, different speaker.

This is why the method's central diagnostic — *how do you feel toward this part?* — measures none of the activation's intensity. It measures the relationship to the activation. "I feel curious about it" indicates Self-energy; "I hate it" is another part answering, and the work moves there first. The question is a regime test.

The method's arc runs through relationship. Work begins not with the exile but with the protectors around it: the person meets each protector, learns its role and its fears, and — over repeated visits — builds enough *trust* that the protector grants permission for contact with what it guards. Only then is the exile approached. *Witnessing* is the heart of the contact: the exile shows what it carries — the images, sensations, and beliefs of the original time — until it feels fully seen and understood, often for the first time. Then *unburdening*: the part is invited to release what it took on, often through spontaneous ritual imagery — giving the burden up to light, water, fire. Freed of it, the part takes up a role of its own choosing, and the protectors, their job obsolete, renegotiate theirs. Trust is the sequence's medium, not a preliminary to it, and its pace sets the pace of the therapy.

## 3. Parts as frozen identity (C1)

What must a part be, for capture to be possible? Whatever took over on that sidewalk was not a fact, because facts can be doubted, and it was not a memory, because memories are had by someone. It arrived, instead, as the one doing the having.

A part, on the present account, is a *bundle*: a set of priors that learned together, under the same pressure, and thereafter reactivate together. Four elements make it up. A *self-state* prior: who I am. *World-state* priors: what this situation is. *Policy* priors: what to do about it. And *expected outcomes*: how this ends. Object-relations theory described the same anatomy — self-image and object-image bound together by affect (Kernberg, 1976) — at a different level of description; the formalism lets us state it as machinery.

The self-state prior deserves its own definition, because the account turns on it. By *self-state* we mean the system's standing answer to a question every agent must keep answered: *what kind of agent is here?* Its content is qualitative — beliefs about capacity (*I cannot take care of myself*), about standing (*I do not matter*), about relational position (*I am alone with this*). Formally it is a context rather than an image: the latent state on which the bundle's other elements condition. And in a burdened bundle, its content is not arbitrary. The formation scene is transcribed into it. Overwhelm, written as content, reads *small*; the failure of every available policy reads *helpless*; the absence of anyone who came reads *alone*. What the moment was becomes who the part is. (Beliefs about worth — *I am bad* — often arrive by one further inference, the one that keeps a needed caregiver good by locating the fault at home; Fairbairn, 1952.)

The dog scene shows the bundle being written. The child learned something about dogs, something about herself, something about what to do, and something about how such moments end — not separately but as a single piece, because they were inferred together, as one explanation of one unbearable afternoon. That is why they return together. And because one of the four elements is a *who*, the bundle does not present itself, on reactivation, as content to a self standing outside it — it supplies the self. Activation delivers the observer along with the observed. Capture is seamless from the inside because there is no one left over to notice it happening.

The four elements are not equals. We propose the self-state prior is the bundle's *root*: the others inherit from it. What a situation means depends on who you take yourself to be; the same hallway is one place to someone who expects to cope, and another place entirely to someone who knows — with the certainty of a child's knowledge — that they cannot. Meaning, policy, and expected outcome all condition on the self-state, and they update, or fail to update, with it. The root position carries an empirical commitment that will matter later: self-states are shared across situations in a way that cue-bound threat meanings are not. Revision that reaches the root should *travel* — to other cues, other fears, situations never treated. Revision that reaches only the threat meaning should stay where it was made.

In what sense is the bundle *frozen*? Freezing has a time-course, and it runs along a continuum. At the slow end, nothing dramatic ever happens. A prior gains precision simply by being used: each activation deposits another count, each count makes the next activation a little more likely, and across years of chronic misattunement — a childhood in which no single day would be worth reporting — a self-state hardens the way a path hardens, by being walked on. At the fast end there is the single overwhelming event, encoded at something near the maximal learning rate, laying down in an afternoon the precision that repetition would have needed a decade to build. Most parts form somewhere between: many faint impressions, and a few searing ones.

The invariant across the continuum is the condition under which the precision is written. Call it *collapsed reflexivity*. In an aware system, one's own state can re-enter the model as something *had* — I notice that I am afraid — rather than only operating as the model — I am simply in a fearful world (Laukkonen & Chandaria, 2024). Overwhelm takes this reflexive re-entry out first: under acute threat, the knowing-that-I-know collapses, and inference narrows to first-order content. The bundle is therefore written into the system while no one, so to speak, is watching. It is installed as reality, not as a representation within one — transparent from the outset, in the sense we develop in §7. And this, not mere strength, is what *frozen* means here. A merely strong prior can still be examined; a frozen one is what examination happens inside of. Frozen is precision acquired below the threshold at which the bundle could be seen.

This ontology has been arrived at before, from directions independent of ours — a triangulation worth recording, not a foundation we rest on. Gendlin (1964), asking why some of what a person carries changes in the course of living while some of it never changes at all, located the difference in *manner* rather than content: experiencing can, in given respects, stop being a process, leaving "only this bare outline… lacking the myriad of fresh detail of the present" — a "frozen whole" that "will not give up its structure" (§13). His explanation is recognizably ours, without the formalism: "Since within the bare structured frozen whole experiencing does not function in interaction with present detail, the structure is not modified by the present. Hence, it remains the same, it repeats itself in many situations without ever changing" (§13). And a living clinical lineage keeps finding the same shape: March (2021, p. 6) sets parts as "structurally consolidated emotional learnings" against fluid process, observing that "what is arising freshly in the unfolding process of the present moment never exactly matches existing structurally consolidated emotional learnings." Neither writer had a precision formalism; both describe frozenness as *being out of interaction with the present* — which is precisely what the precision account derives. One translation is owed to Gendlin: he resisted treating experience as stored content at all — structure, for him, was a process that stopped, not a belief that persists — so our rendering of the frozen whole as a high-precision prior is a reconstruction he might have declined. What we take from the convergence is its vote of confidence, and one increment that is C1's own: a part is a frozen whole whose content is *identity*.

The bundle's nearest neighbors, for contrast, contain no *who*. A *schema* is content that updates while the person remains who they are. A *latent context* selects which model is operative, not who the agent is within it: a context switch says *now use model B*; a part activation says *now I am the self that model B was organized around*. A *trait prior* is a slowly updated disposition, not an identity state coupled to world-meaning and policy in a single reactivating unit. Because the bundle contains a who, C1 predicts three things its neighbors do not. Activation arrives as a whole world rather than a single belief, because the four elements arrive together. Identity-level revision generalizes while threat-level revision stays local, because the root is shared and the meanings are cue-bound. And activation resumes an earlier self-position rather than recalling an earlier memory — a difference invisible during capture, because capture supplies the observer, and conspicuous in retrospect.

Frozen — and, later, melting — are descriptions; the mechanism is the precision dynamics of the sections that follow. The words add ontology, not machinery. And the referent of the ontology is the burdened configuration, not the person's capacities: when a part melts, it will have demonstrated that it was revisable — not that it never existed. What melts is the coupling; what the coupling bound persists. What this implies about IFS's own ontology of parts, including where we depart from it, is the business of §10.

## 4. How a part freezes

Not every frightening thing becomes a part. Two children are knocked down by the same dog; one is frightened, comforted, and within weeks is playing with dogs again; the other acquires something that will still be organizing her behavior thirty years later. A theory of parts owes an account of the difference, and IFS's clinical answer — that it is not the threat that matters but the overwhelm (Schwartz & Sweezy, 2020) — points toward it. We propose that two conditions jointly set formation apart from ordinary aversive learning: *overwhelm* and *low control*. Overwhelm is precision-weighted prediction error beyond what the system's existing causes can assimilate — not merely surprising input, but input no explanation on hand can absorb. Control is the gradient of expected free energy across available policies: there is control when some action is expected to make things better, and low control when that landscape is flat — when nothing the child can do is expected to help. Threat alone, with control intact, produces fear learning of the ordinary, revisable kind. It is threat *plus* the flat landscape that freezes.

Under these joint limits the system still updates — overwhelmed learning is still learning, and at maximal intensity. But the update, we argue, carries three traits, and that together they make its product not merely strong but frozen.

First, the update *spawns a new cause* rather than revising an old one. When error is too large to be absorbed into any existing explanation, the rational move is not to bend a parameter but to infer a new latent cause to carry the experience (Gershman, Blei, & Niv, 2010; Gershman et al., 2017). In a single step, a slice of the world receives its own dedicated model. This, we propose, is the formal event behind the clinical intuition that a part is *born* — and it is where the welding happens: the new cause is fit to one scene, all at once, which is why its four elements move as a unit ever after. (The spawn also sharpens the contrast with latent contexts: latent-cause inference describes how any new cause comes to exist; what makes this cause a part is its content — it contains a who.)

Second, the new cause is *encoded transparently*. Collapsed reflexivity is the condition: under overwhelm, the knowing-that-I-know collapses first, so the cause is laid down with no vantage point outside it — unconscious, in roughly the ordinary sense of the word.

Third, the belief is *written without ever being tested*. Low control means no policy is expected to reduce the error, so the system cannot act on the world to probe what it has just learned. The belief is written at high precision by a system that, in the same moment, cannot generate its own counter-evidence. Trauma, on this account, is maximal write with zero test.

At the far corner of the two conditions — overwhelm extreme, control absent — sits shutdown. We propose to read it not as an autonomic primitive imported into the model, but as this corner of the model's own formation variables: when no overt action is expected to help, the one move remaining is covert: attenuate precision itself, turn down the gain on what cannot be borne (mental action in the sense of Sandved-Smith et al., 2021; cf. the active inference reading of dissociation, Deane et al., 2020). Overwhelm sets the magnitude of the moment; control selects the response to it: mobilize while some action still bites, attenuate once none does. The same attenuation regime returns later as dissociative quiet mistaken for calm, and as a control condition in simulation; we model its inferential signature only. Its bodily implementation — dorsal collapse (Porges, 2011), and the re-enactment of that collapse when a shutdown-formed part is later activated — enters the account as clinical description, not as a variable.

What formation writes, persistence keeps, and the keeping requires no keeper. The frozen bundle maintains itself by a loop with three strokes. Its priors are precise, so present context is underweighted — Gendlin's *not in interaction with present detail*, now as a precision statement. Its policies are avoidant, so the situations that could disconfirm it are never sampled: the evidence that would melt the bundle is exactly the evidence its own policy prevents. And a prior over policies gains precision simply by being used, decoupled from whether its use remains optimal (Friston et al., 2016; Proietti et al., 2025) — the path hardens because it is walked. The loop seals itself.

Two predictions fall out, one restrictive and one inclusive, and both match the clinical picture. Not all fear becomes a part: high threat with preserved control — the child who could run, who was chased but reached the gate, who was caught and then found — yields ordinary fear learning, revisable by ordinary means. And chronic neglect is part-forming without any event worth the name: at the slow end of the continuum, overwhelm and low control are ambient rather than acute, and the same three traits accrue by slow accumulation rather than in one strike. The account also says what the burden will be: the formation conditions write themselves in as content — the transcription, now visible as mechanism. A part believes what its formation was.

What could revise such a structure? Nothing in the loop, by construction. And the difficulty is about to compound, because the system does not leave a frozen bundle unattended. What it builds around the wound is the next section's subject; what could finally melt the whole arrangement occupies the three sections after that.

## 5. The protective system

IFS presents its taxonomy — exiles holding the burdens, protectors managing the system away from them — as clinical observation. In the present model it is a derivation. An exile is a bundle frozen around an overwhelmed self-state, and activating it is expensive: it brings back prediction error the system has already failed once to resolve, and, under low Self-energy (the condition §7 makes precise), it costs the system its current reality model. A system minimizing expected free energy will therefore learn to steer wide of the exile's activation conditions: acting on the forecast rather than the event. But this learning, by itself, is ordinary. It is system-level policy learning — dynamic, revisable, updated as contexts change — and much of everyday avoidance never becomes anything more. Avoidance is not yet a protector, because a policy is not yet a *who*.

A protector is born, we propose, the way every part is born: under formation conditions, arising on the protection side of the ledger. The clearest case is the breakthrough. Avoidance fails; the exile is accessed; the flood arrives. And the flood is itself an overwhelm the system cannot control, a re-run of the formation scene at one remove. Whatever coping is live in that emergency — the dissociation, the rage, the binge, the desperate charm — is welded, under the same overwhelm and the same absence of control, to a self-state transcribing the moment: *I am the only thing that stands between us and that.* This is a firefighter: a part whose formation event was an emergency, and which believes what its formation was — hence the urgency, and the grandiosity of role, that firefighters present with. Managers more often form at the slow end of the continuum: a childhood spent managing a volatile parent is ambient overwhelm with ambient low control, and the vigilant appeaser is laid down across a thousand unremarkable evenings rather than struck in one. Repetition thereafter does what repetition does. But use explains a protector's rigidity, not its birth. What use alone builds is habit; only formation conditions write a who. The distinction matters clinically: a habit updates when circumstances change and yields to skills and practice, while a protector must be negotiated with — because one of them has beliefs about what it is protecting, and the other is not anyone at all. Managers and firefighters, then, are the prospective and reactive faces of the same forecast — the manager acts so that the forecast never comes due; the firefighter acts once it has — and their characteristic time-courses differ because their formation events do.

It matters that these are parts and not mechanisms. A protector is a full bundle, all four elements present. It has a self-state of its own — *I am the one who keeps us composed*. It has a model of its charge: beliefs about what happens if the exile comes forward. It has policies — suppress, perfect, numb, charm, scan — and it has feared outcomes: humiliation, flooding, abandonment. The *gate*, by contrast, is not a part at all. Whether access to the exile is currently open, closed, or partial is simply the net output of whichever protector policies are running. The distinction does clinical work: IFS negotiates with protectors rather than dismantling defenses, and the model says why the negotiation is addressed to the right kind of thing — a gate has no beliefs to update; a part does. Policy-level intervention can suppress or retrain a behavior; what it cannot do, on this account, is change the protector's mind.

Protection also stratifies, and it does so by the same fork as formation. Protectors are strategies deployed into a changing world, and strategies age. A child's protest, a teenager's silence, an adult's charm: each was fit to a context that did not hold still. When a protector's strategy begins to fail — or worse, begins to cost, because the protest is punished, the silence reads as coldness, the charm attracts the wrong attention — the system faces the same fork formation posed, and it is resolved, we suggest, by the same variables: the magnitude of the error, and whether anything can be done about it. If the mismatch is assimilable, the protector *revises* — it learns a new policy mapping, the strategy softens or reshapes, and no new structure forms. If the mismatch overwhelms — the strategy itself becomes the occasion of punishment or shame, and there is nothing the child can do about that either — a new cause is spawned on top: a second protector, developmentally later, whose role now includes gating the first.

The revising branch, common as it is, has a strict scope: what updates is the policy mapping — the bundle's interface with the world — and not the self-state at its root. The asymmetry is structural. Strategies act constantly, so they receive constant evidence about *what works*; the mandate — who I am, what I stand between us and — receives none, because nothing in the protector's own operation ever samples it. Its avoidance keeps the mandate untested, and its transparency keeps it from ever being an object of revision at all. A protector updates its methods, never its mission. The result is a familiar clinical signature: the polished, thoroughly contemporary manager who, asked what would happen if it stopped, answers in the voice of a four-year-old. The tactics are current; the mandate is frozen at formation. Flexibility at the interface, then, is no evidence against a frozen root: a protector's strategy may adapt fluidly while its mandate never moves. And it completes the fork: assimilable mismatch remaps policy within the part; overwhelming mismatch spawns a new layer; and the root revises by neither route — only, as §8 will argue, by evidence of a different kind.

Protection therefore stratifies. Each layer encodes the self-state of its era and a strategy fit to a vanished context, and each gates access to the layer beneath it once that layer's strategy is no longer safe to run. The result is the layered protective system IFS meets clinically from the outside in.

The architecture is now complete: a wound, frozen; around it, layers of frozen management, each fit to a context that has since vanished. And notice that nothing in it can revise itself. Every layer is self-sealing by the formation loop, and the layers seal one another — the outer protectors keep the inner ones from running, the inner ones keep the exile from speaking — and when the sealing fails, the arrangement does not revise; it thickens, by the fork above: another breakthrough, another layer. What the system lacks is not information — the corrective facts have usually been available for years — but a position from which any of this could be *seen* rather than seen through. The next three sections are about that position: what it is and what governs its availability, the two regimes it separates, and what becomes possible within it.

## 6. Self and Self-energy (C2)

IFS holds that the missing position is not another part. Beneath the parts, on its account, there is someone home: a Self that needs no development, cannot be damaged, and relates to parts with qualities no part supplies. Stated that way the claim sounds like doctrine, and it has sometimes been read, inside IFS and out, as metaphysics. We propose to read it as architecture.

Self, on the present account, names two things at once. The first is a *regime*: the mode of inference in which no bundle's precision dominates. Activation may be present, even strong; no part's priors are setting the terms of the whole. The second is a *content*: the present-moment self-state that self-modeling yields when that regime obtains. It is the system's current answer to *what kind of agent is here* — computed fresh, not supplied by a frozen root. The two are not independent. The content is available exactly when the regime obtains; under capture, the bundle's transcript stands in for it. One variable governs both.

That variable, we propose, is *epistemic depth* (Laukkonen & Chandaria, 2024): the degree to which the system's model is reflexively shared with itself — reflexivity precision; write it E_t. To know *a part of me is afraid*, rather than only *I am afraid*, is to have one's current state in the model as something had, not only operative as the model. This is collapsed reflexivity, met again as a live variable rather than a formation condition. And the identification earns its keep, because reflexivity precision is, formally, precision on a recursive loop: the model's own global state re-entering the system as evidence. A construct like that has two faces by construction — the weighting of the loop, and what the loop carries. Those are the regime-face and the content-face of Self. The weighting is Laukkonen and Chandaria's construct as published; reading what the loop carries as evidence available to an activated part is ours, and we propose it as such. Our second claim is the clinical setting. Self-energy is epistemic depth *under activation*: reflexivity precision maintained while a bundle is live — held exactly where it collapsed at formation. Depth in the meditation hall is one achievement; depth while the frozen is running is the one therapy turns on.

Read this way, the doctrine of Self stops being doctrine. Everyone has Self, because a regime is architectural — a standing possibility of any hierarchical inference system, nothing to acquire and nothing to build. Self is not a part, as a matter of kind: parts are bundles; Self is a mode of organization among them. Self needs access rather than development, because what stands between a person and the regime is never a missing skill but an occluding precision. And Self cannot be damaged — the article that sounds most like faith — because trauma freezes contents, and a regime is not a content. Capture can occlude it, hold it out of reach for years. There is nothing there to injure. Even Self-*leadership* reads naturally: action selected from the regime rather than compelled by a bundle. What the doctrine of essence was protecting, the architecture provides. The derivations are cheap, deliberately so: they show that nothing more than a regime is needed. The substantive claim is the identification itself — that Self is the regime — and it is a proposal, evidenced by how much doctrine it recovers. The reification — treating the capacity as a thing — adds nothing but risk, a caution the contemplative lineages raise from their own side (March, 2021). On what parts are, this paper departs from IFS orthodoxy; on what Self is, it arrives at orthodoxy by derivation.

Many of the eight C's — IFS's phenomenological signature of Self — fall out of the regime the same way. Curiosity is the epistemic drive with no bundle foreclosing it: a system free to find out, because no prior already knows how this ends. Calm is precision no longer monopolized by a threat forecast. Clarity is evidence weighted as evidence. Confidence is an open policy space — action available rather than compelled. These are not qualities added to inference; they are what inference is like when nothing frozen is running it. Not all of the C's reduce so transparently, and we do not pretend otherwise. Compassion, in particular, we do not claim to derive: a sketch exists in adjacent work — unbounded free-energy minimisation once no boundary is privileged (Sandved-Smith et al., 2026, §6.2) — and we cite it as convergence rather than result. The identification we propose is for the epistemic core of Self-energy; the warmth that clinicians rightly treat as its hallmark is either downstream of the regime, as such derivations suggest, or an ingredient the present account has not reduced.

The availability of depth is conditioned from below. Ventral-vagal availability, embodied safety, co-regulation (Porges, 2011): these set how much reflexivity precision is actually available under activation — sympathetic threat collapses it, as it did at formation. The nervous system enters this account as an input, not a component: described clinically, never modeled. And it follows that Self-energy is often *borrowed before it is owned*. A client may arrive at a session blended, seeing the room through the part's eyes. The therapist does not argue with it — she stands steady in her own Self and brings curiosity, compassion, and co-regulation. Eventually something in the client's system registers, and the room is seen through adult eyes again. The therapist's steadiness is external scaffolding for a depth the client's system cannot yet hold alone.

And quiet is not depth. A system can be behaviorally calm because attenuation is running — the shutdown corner deployed as defense: dissociative quiet. The discriminator is whether present evidence stays online. Self-led calm is wide open — the room, the body, the other person all still landing. Dissociative quiet is closed, and the body's very stillness is the tell. The converse imposter also exists: fluent parts-language delivered while the body is in threat — depth reported rather than had, reflexivity precision absent exactly where it counts, under activation. IFS calls this a self-like part; the present account gives it a precise signature, and returns to it as a hard case in §10.

Everything now turns on how E_t enters inference — and on what evidence becomes available once it does.

## 7. Capture and context-held activation (C2)

Return to the sidewalk, and let the moment go two ways. In the first, the jolt arrives and the world is simply the part's world: the dog is danger, the body is small, the street is a corridor to escape from — and only afterwards, if at all, *what came over me?* In the second, the jolt arrives and something else remains true at the same time: the fear is here, fully live, and so is the street, the ordinary dog, the adult body, the knowledge that one is standing in daylight decades later. Same bundle, same activation. The difference between the two is a regime difference, and everything in the therapy turns on it.

We propose that E_t enters inference in exactly one place: the precision balance between bundle priors and present evidence. As depth rises, the bundle's *effective* precision falls and present context's rises — formally, π_eff = r_t · π_part · e^(−βE_t) against λ_eff = λ_ctx · e^(+γE_t), where π_part and λ_ctx are the learned precisions of bundle and present context, r_t is the part's current cue-driven activation, and β, γ set how strongly depth tips the balance. The capture index C_t = π_eff / (π_eff + λ_eff) says how much of inference the bundle currently owns. Capture is therefore graded, not binary — one can be mostly gone and partly here. The mechanism is the paper's only one: nothing else toggles, gates, or switches; what follow are consequences. And it acts on *effective* precision — the gain on the frozen structure at inference time — not on the structure itself, a distinction §8 will need.

The one mechanism has two consequences, and they matter differently. The first is informational: as C_t falls, ordinary present evidence regains its weight — the actual dog, the actual distances, the body's actual size. The second is relational: among the things present context contains is the system's own present-moment self-state — the content-face of Self. When capture is high, that state is discounted along with everything else present. When capture falls, it becomes observable *to the activated part*: evidence, available for the first time, about who is here with it. No channel opens; nothing is switched on. The same falling gain that lets the street back in lets the part register that it is not alone on it.

The regime change has an experiential face. Under capture the bundle is *transparent*: perceived through, functioning as the architecture of the moment rather than as anything in it (Metzinger, 2003; Limanowski & Friston, 2018). As depth rises it *opacifies* — it becomes an object, something with a felt relation to it, a part of me rather than the whole of me. IFS's clinical shorthand names the transition: speaking *from* the part versus speaking *for* it.

Call the second way the moment can go *context-held activation*: the part live, capture prevented. It is not distraction, which keeps context by dropping the activation; not suppression, which fights the activation with another policy; not dissociation, which quiets the scene by attenuating evidence altogether. And the clinical probe now reads as what it is. *How do you feel toward this part?* is a regime test: "I hate it" means another bundle has the floor — capture by proxy; "I feel curious about it" means the bundle is opacified and someone is home. The question measures C_t, in the only units the therapy room has.

Why is this regime the one in which lasting change is possible? Because revision needs three things at once: the part *active*, so its priors are live and error can reach them; present context *online*, so there is corrective evidence to register; and capture *absent*, so the evidence is weighted rather than discounted. Capture fails the third condition — the corrective facts are present and worthless. Calm-without-activation fails the first — there is nothing live to revise, which is why insight gathered in comfortable sessions so often changes nothing. Dissociative quiet fails the second — the evidence itself is turned down. Witnessing, as IFS practices it, is context-held activation deliberately cultivated and *held* — and what the holding is for, what evidence does the revising once the window is open, is the next section's question.

## 8. Relational revision (C3)

Consider a session deep into the work. Weeks have gone to the protectors, and they have given permission. Now the client turns inward, and the exile shows the scene it has carried: the cornered child; the door no one came through. The client stays with it. Nothing about the scene is corrected. Nobody explains that dogs are safe, or that the past is over. And yet something moves — the thing that years of accurate information never moved. Clients relay the moment from the inside, in words so uniform that IFS trainers treat them as a landmark. The part says: *she sees me now.* What evidence did that?

None of this is confined to the therapy room. The same window opens in self-led practice, in a friendship, in a marriage — anywhere depth is held while a part is live. The session is simply where it is easiest to watch.

We propose that the load-bearing content of the frozen root is relational: *I am alone with this*. That is what the formation scene wrote — no one came, and the absence became content. An expectation like that is not tested by facts about dogs. It is tested by one thing only: how the part is met while it shows what it carries. Witnessed contact is that test. Capture is low, so the part can register the system's present-moment self-state — the content-face — for the first time. And what it registers contradicts its oldest prediction. Not *the dog is safe*; the bundle has discounted that error for decades, and avoidance has kept it from arriving anyway. *I am not alone with this.* Once contact occurs, this error arrives through a channel the bundle cannot seal, because the contact itself is the evidence. It lands directly on the self-state prior, at the root.

When we say the part *registers* contact, we mean that its relational expectations — priors about how it will be met — receive their precision-weighted error. When a part *sees*, *believes*, or *feels seen*, here and throughout, the same translation applies: the agentive idiom is compression, not commitment. What it compresses is always an update landing on a bundle.

Gendlin (1964) reached this conclusion from phenomenology alone. Structure-bound experience cannot be carried forward by the person carrying it: "the individual cannot respond to himself" in the respects that are frozen; "responses are needed first" (§§15, 19). Another's response reconstitutes a process where, before it, there was no process. He saw the targeting constraint too: respond to the *functioning* experiencing, never to the structure (§20). You do not unfreeze a frozen whole by arguing with it. You meet what is still alive around it. The present account adds one move Gendlin did not make, and the move is what makes IFS possible: *another* is bundle-relative, not skin-relative. Self stands outside the bundle while inside the person. The regime is the one position no part occupies. So the response that reconstitutes can come from within, once depth is available — and when it cannot, early or under load, the therapist is the responder. Self-energy is borrowed before it is owned. IFS practice honors the sequence: direct access first if needed, then the client's Self returned to the part as soon as it can hold.

The same signal, aimed one level up, changes protectors. A protector's forecast is also a relational expectation: contact with the exile brings flooding, humiliation, collapse. Witnessed contact tests it too. The catastrophe is predicted. The contact happens. The catastrophe does not arrive. The cost estimate updates, and access relaxes a notch. IFS calls this building trust, and the account gives trust a referent: the part's running relational prior about Self — its learned forecast of what contact yields. Trust is slow because it is built the only way a frozen prior can be revised: repeated evidence, under the held regime. It is tested actively — parts provoke, withhold, show a little and watch — and the testing is sampling, not stubbornness. And it is asymmetric. A single misattunement confirms the old world; each contact that contradicts it is one count against thousands. Strategy feedback never reaches the mandate; this does. The same operation that heals the exile befriends the protector.

The regime and the revision run on different clocks. Raising E_t moves *effective* precision — fast, reversible, gone by dinnertime. A moment of witnessing melts nothing. But while the regime is held, the structural update runs. Relational error drains the frozen self-state prior's accuracy, contact by contact. Eventually the coupling that welds the bundle no longer pays for its complexity, and Bayesian model reduction removes it (Friston et al., 2017). The prune is selective. The weld goes. The competences it bound persist at ordinary precision, released — melting ice does not delete the water. This is why deep change, prepared slowly, so often completes suddenly: reduction is a discrete event, a model losing to a simpler one, not a dial turning down. And it is why the part afterwards is not gone but different. The released capacities re-organize under Self-led policy, and the role becomes chosen where it was compulsive. (Freezing spawned a dedicated cause; melting prunes the burden it carried. We offer the symmetry as a reading, at the same interpretive register as the reduction story itself. The destabilize-and-revise shape is compatible with reconsolidation accounts; whether that machinery is engaged is an empirical question.)

*Witnessing* is the held state: context-held activation, sustained. It names the regime, never the structural event. *Melting* is the structural sequence that runs while the state is held — revision, then release. Sustained witnessing, we propose, is sufficient: nothing beyond the held regime and the evidence it admits is required. This is why traditions with no unburdening step report the same endpoint: a part met rightly releases and unfolds (March, 2021). *Unburdening* is IFS's name for the melt's completion, and its procedural invitation of it. The ritual reads naturally as prompted model reduction — an explicit invitation to evaluate whether the burden still earns its complexity. It is fact-free in the sense of Friston et al. (2017), requiring no new evidence, and this matches its character: imaginal, non-informational, performed after the evidential work is done. The reading yields a prediction the clinical lore already carries. Unburden too early, while the burden still contributes accuracy, and the invited reduction loses the comparison. The part takes the burden back. The rule — witness fully before unburdening — is not etiquette. It is the condition under which the prune can run.

Applied recursively, this is the whole therapy. Access to the exile waits on the protectors' forecasts updating. Each witnessed layer relaxes the gate to the next. The gate state, at any moment, is the running readout of accumulated trust. Protectors before exiles; permission before contact; months where the work seems to circle — the ordering falls out of the architecture. Witnessing remains a state. The *descent* is the trajectory repeated witnessing traces down the stack. And when the root finally revises, the change is not confined to dogs, because what revised is the prior that every situation shared. The person who was *always alone with it* is not, now, anywhere. Generalization beyond the treated cue is the account's signature prediction. It is also the reason the deepest changes read, from inside, less like learning something and more like becoming someone.

## 9. The formalism running

The claims are implemented in a suite of discrete active inference simulations: Dirichlet-count learning over factored states, expected free energy for policy selection, latent-cause structure learning for formation, Bayesian model reduction for the melt, and E_t entering inference at the single point §7 specified. The suite was built adversarially. Every experiment ran twice — a pilot on ten worlds, then, with every rule and constant frozen, a confirmatory run on twenty worlds the code had never seen — with matched controls, preregistered thresholds, and an external adversarial review between cycles. Claims that failed were recorded as failures and retained in the registries beside their replacements; several of the results below exist only because their first versions died. One register note carries through everything: where a mechanism was shaped during piloting and then reproduced on fresh worlds, the result shows that this architecture reliably produces this pattern — a demonstration of coherence, not a confirmation of a prior prediction. These are existence proofs and prediction generators, not clinical evidence.

The first simulation sweeps the formation conditions, and it earns them twice — once against a matched control, once against the account we expected. A latent-cause agent meets an aversive world at every combination of overwhelm and control, and the same worlds are run again as exact replay, identical events with the loop from action to exposure severed. In the closed loop, freezing lives at high overwhelm and *partial* control, and releases as control rises; under replay, control changes nothing — the same cells freeze at every efficacy, because efficacy can only matter through the loop. At the helpless edge the agent does not freeze at all: with no action that works, nothing seals the belief against the world, and when the world turns safe the agent notices. Freezing requires avoidance that succeeds — succeeds well enough to keep the wound from ever being retested. And the signature is capture, not accumulation: the closed-loop agent receives roughly a third of the replay agent's aversive evidence and revises half as much, the opposite of what sheer count mass predicts, with the effective-precision tilt of §7 as the measured mediator. Working avoidance removes the later, expected, transparently-written evidence, so the collapsed writes stay dominant — the self-sealing loop of §4, running as count arithmetic. Attenuation, meanwhile, tracks helplessness and not hazard: selected along the no-control edge at every severity, exactly zero once efficacy crosses a modest floor. The chronic slow route into the frozen phase appeared in most fresh worlds but below its preregistered rate, and stands recorded at that weaker strength.

The second simulation isolates what melting requires, and returned one derivation and one correction. The derivation is §7's central claim as machinery: the reduction comparison is computed over what the system can reflexively access, and under transparency it has nothing to evaluate — witnessing changes the regime in which evidence can reach the comparison, while moving no structural count itself. Remove that accessibility premise and grant the same evidence at write time, and the melt fails; the premise is load-bearing, not decorative. The correction is ours to own: we expected relational content to be the only evidence that melts, and the fresh-seed run falsified it — informational safe evidence, once the route is open, revises comparably. What the relationship uniquely supplies, on this suite's showing, is not a species of evidence but the opening itself. The model also generated a prediction we did not ask of it: relational evidence arriving under capture is banked at a discounted weight that revision cannot yet use, so contact that seems to accomplish nothing may be loading the comparison that later depth completes.

The third simulation carries the claim spine, and survived everything we ran at it. A two-factor architecture — shared self-state root, cue-bound threat meanings — is trained on one cue and probed on untreated ones. Under witnessing, revision reaches identity before threat in every one of twenty fresh worlds; under matched exposure, never; with the architecture reversed — threat at the root, learning intact, training fit matched — never. The ordering is not a magnitude that could be tuned into existence; it is a sequence, and only one arrangement of the graph produces it.

The fourth simulation gives the dyad its division of labor, with the co-regulation channel learned rather than granted. The therapist enters as two channels — surface coherence and the state of the relational body — and the client learns, from contingency alone, what each configuration does to its own settling. The learned mapping carries the signature: the fluent-but-threatened therapist of §6 separates from both the regulated and the dysregulated one in the client's acquired model; reverse the world's contingencies and the signature reverses; degrade them and it degrades; lesion the learned counts mid-course and it collapses — the discrimination lives in what was learned, nowhere else. And the division of labor is now earned in both directions, because contact generates root evidence whatever the content: regulation alone lowered capture by a quarter and revised nothing, in twenty of twenty fresh worlds. Co-regulation changes the regime; evidence walks through it; neither substitutes for the other.

Depth itself, in these simulations, is not a dial but an inferred state, and here the record divides cleanly between a landscape and a fall. The landscape is robust: in the continuous model, Self and capture stand as competing basins across a single connected region spanning the full range of the tilt slopes, and the capture basin ceases to exist above a threshold of baseline felt safety — enough ground and there is nothing to fall into. The fall is another matter. Under autonomous drive — depth excursions generated independently of the agent's state — no trajectory crossed from Self into capture, in either the continuous or the discrete model; the collapse signature also proved non-specific to the theory's volatility mapping once the observation schedule was decoupled. Every fall this suite has ever produced ran through a loop — action shaping exposure, avoidance protecting the wound. We advance it as a hypothesis rather than a result, since no experiment yet adds and removes the same loop in the same collapse model: on this architecture's showing, capture is entered by enactment, not by drift.

The protective system's ordering — protectors before exiles, permission before contact — is the account's hardest claim, and we report it at the register it has earned: not yet. Grown stacks with the ordering read out rather than encoded failed three preregistered cycles; the diagnosis, confirmed by an identifying experiment, is architectural — a model in which one part is active at a time cannot form the coupling the ordering needs, because a protector never learns on a live exile. An exploratory architecture in which parts observe parts — each cause learning what the world is like when another is active, through the same count arithmetic as everything else — grows the coupling in some worlds and descends a four-layer stack outside-in in the cleanest of them, with the ordering destroyed by shuffling the learned coupling and absent without the gate; in other worlds the same rule grows the coupling backwards, which is what a readout rather than an axiom looks like. The rebuilt version of that architecture, reduced to ordinary machinery with no free gains, then produced the program's most striking derivation by failing: its therapy deadlocks, because a gated part is never contacted and so never relaxes its gate, and because contact that makes a protector's own world safer makes approaching the exile compare *worse* against leaving things be. The arithmetic arrived at the clinical axiom on its own — a part system cannot open itself from inside; permission is granted to something outside the system, or not at all. Coupling the learned therapist-trust of the dyad simulation into that gate is the sequel's specified first task, joined to the older open problem of scoring spawn proposals against the hyper-model's state so that formation under inferred depth can run in either direction.

One further simulation threads a single agent through an entire biography — childhood formation, adult years, therapy, probe — as one continuously mutated state, no stage boundary touching a belief. What the lived version keeps is the account's spine: the write-time reflexivity of childhood is the capture of adulthood, the same quantity evolved rather than recomputed; adult outcomes are sharply bimodal; and in every captured life, witnessing melts what the biography froze. What it does not yet deliver is the rest: capture prevalence missed its preregistered gate, the reversed-graph control was not distinguished by raw adversity prediction — direction discriminates only through relational transfer, as the third simulation found — and the fixed probe confounds bank growth with frozenness. The one-life result, like the ordering, is a mechanism demonstrated and a claim still owed.

What the suite shows, then, is smaller than the architecture of §§3–8 and harder to take away. Freezing requires a loop and partial control; revision requires access, and access is what the relationship supplies; identity revises before threat, and only one direction of the graph produces that; regime and evidence are separately necessary; the two basins exist, and enough felt safety abolishes one of them. What it does not show is the protective ordering, a spontaneous fall, a unique relational species of evidence, or that human change works this way. The confirmatory tests are §10's.

## 10. Discussion

The identity-level signature the paper opened with now has an explanation at each of its faces. Activation arrives as a whole world because four priors arrive as one bundle. It announces itself only in retrospect because the bundle is transparent and supplies the observer. Not every fear becomes a part, and neglect without any event does, because freezing requires overwhelm and low control — in one strike or by slow accumulation. Protection exists, stratifies, and modernizes its strategies without ever updating its mandate, because cost forecasts crystallize under the same formation conditions and only the policy interface receives ordinary evidence. Calm without contact changes nothing, contact without depth changes nothing, and dissociative quiet mimics calm while changing nothing — three failures of the three conditions revision requires. Witnessing changes things suddenly, generalizes beyond the treated cue, and must reach protectors first, because relational error revises a shared root behind gates whose forecasts update in order. The clinical shape of IFS — protectors first, permission before contact, witness fully before unburdening — falls out of the architecture at every step.

The confirmatory tests are not simulations but studies, and the predictions are specific enough to fail. Under witnessing, identity-level content should revise before threat-level content; under matched exposure, the order should reverse (C1+C2). Change from witnessing should transfer to structurally similar untreated fears — the generalization gradient — while change from contact alone stays cue-bound (C3). Protector relaxation, measurable as willingness to allow contact, should precede exile-level revision in IFS-style work and show no such ordering under exposure. Unburdening attempted before witnessing completes should fail in a characteristic way — the burden returns — and the failure should track remaining evidential work, not ritual quality. And the habit–protector line should be drawable in vivo: avoidances that answer the probe with a voice should require negotiation and resist skills training, while avoidances that answer with nothing should yield to practice — with a further signature one level down, since strategy-level change without role change marks a protector still in place, and role transformation marks a root revised.

The account departs from IFS orthodoxy at one point, and we state the departure rather than smooth it. Schwartz holds that parts are innate sub-minds that pre-exist their burdens; the present account holds that the burdened configuration is created by the freeze. The disagreement is real, and it is safe to have. It is mechanically inert: formation, capture, witnessing, and melt are the same processes under either reading. It cannot be settled from inside: no finite agent can evidence its own internal boundaries (Sandved-Smith et al., 2026; Fields & Glazebrook, 2023), and a part reporting that it existed before the trauma is testimony generated by the current model — data about the self-narrative, not about pre-existence. And the observation most often cited for innateness is one the account predicts: parts persist, transformed, after unburdening, because the prune removes the coupling and not the capacities. If innateness is nonetheless right, the model absorbs it — dispositional seeds, prepared action systems that the freeze recruits rather than creates — and nothing mechanical changes. What the doctrine protects clinically survives in full on our reading: the part's talents were not manufactured by trauma, and the part merits appreciation, not deletion.

The departure is parts-only. On Self, the account arrives at orthodoxy rather than leaving it: innateness, undamageability, non-part-hood, access-without-development are all properties of a regime.

The contact-only comparisons above are component analyses, not verdicts on exposure therapy. Exposure as practiced includes presence, titration, and regulation — nonzero Self-energy by this paper's own definition — and its effectiveness is not in question; which variable does which work is. Nor is all protection outdated: some protectors run accurate current contingencies, and the model formalizes protection that has outlived its context, not protection as such. Several hard cases are named and not solved. The self-like part has a signature — depth reported without reflexivity precision under activation — but no model. Polarization, multi-part networks, and the dyad are described, not derived. And the agentive idiom itself — what it is, formally, for evidence to arrive *at a bundle* rather than at the system — deserves a treatment we have only gestured at. And the protective stack is stated as architecture while its formal machinery — the gate as an inferred state, per-layer trust variables, the stacked model they require — is deferred to the sequel this account sets up.

Sometimes I am afraid. Sometimes a part of me is afraid. The paper began by claiming that the difference between these sentences is the decisive therapeutic variable, and it can now say what the difference is. In the first sentence, a frozen bundle is the reality model, and no one is left over. In the second, someone is home: depth enough to hold the fear as *a part of me*, and evidence enough — for the part — that it is no longer alone with what it carries. Between the two sentences stands a precision balance; behind the balance, a single variable; through the window it opens, the only error that reaches the root. What froze in isolation melts in relationship.
