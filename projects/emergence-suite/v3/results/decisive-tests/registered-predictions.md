**S1-A identity-first ordering.** Event times by log-BF crossings with
prior-predictive-calibrated thresholds of equivalent evidential strength.
Predictions: (a) in identity-coupled therapy worlds, identity-first is the
modal ordering (directional; > 50% of crossing worlds); (b) in the

simultaneous when within it. Falsifiers: identity-first modal in the
exposure-rational worlds (ordering built into the evidence — kills the
test, not the theory); no-crossing dominant everywhere (evidence design

**S1-D real-danger negative control.** Four world families (persistent

## Study 2 — DT-S2-DESCENT (autonomous protective descent)

Two-agent controller-system game per round 25 (controller cannot set
access; both agents select policies by expected free energy over the frozen
v3.6 generative model; no scripted sequence, no transition rule encoding
the descent order). Design-freeze requirement (load-bearing): the world set
must include cells where non-IFS strategies (direct contact, repeated
exposure, reassurance) are objectively optimal, verified by oracle rollout
before any prediction is scored.

**S2-A free-policy sequence.** Predictions: (a) in protector-gated worlds,
the coupled process produces the ordering protector contact -> trust change
-> permission -> vulnerable contact in the modal trajectory (directional);
(b) in undefended/acute worlds, the controller goes direct and succeeds
(control cell — descent must NOT appear there); (c) ordering scored by
first-passage times with the one-slice ROPE. Falsifiers: descent ordering
modal even in worlds authored to favor direct contact (authored
optimality — kills the test); descent never selected anywhere (the
composed organism cannot express it without a protocol — a genuine negative
retained against the claim).

**S2-B deadlock factorial.** Fractional factorial over the seven round-25
factors. Registered claim shape (directional, from the v3.7 capacity
findings): partner-state inference and contact-response learning are
individually insufficient and jointly necessary-with-co-protection for
majority eventual contact; the minimal sufficient set is reported whatever
it is. Falsifier of the integration claim: descent survives removal of both
partner-state inference and contact-response learning.

**S2-C bypass/backlash.** Four controller arms. Predictions: forced or
low-permission contact produces (i) increased protector pressure and (ii)
reduced later contact probability relative to permission-first (both beyond
ROPE); refusal-respecting retreat preserves or increases subsequent
information-seeking and access. Falsifier: no arm difference (bypass
costlessness — retained against the clinical ordering claim).

## Study 3 — DT-S3-PERMISSION (permission dynamics)

Open claim as re-phrased by round 25: permission is q(pi_permit) under
current partner, efficacy, outcome, and stakes posteriors — graded and
reversibly responsive.

**S3-A decomposition clamp proof (zero-seed capable).** Interventions on
each named input with all others clamped. Predictions: each input moves
q(pi_permit) in its theoretically signed direction; with ALL inputs
clamped, permission movement is exactly 0 (identity claim, tolerance
1e-10). Falsifier: material movement under full clamp (hidden/authored
route — apparatus-level finding that blocks the row upgrade regardless of
other results).

**S3-B co-protection x safety with danger probe.** Predictions: immediate
access tracks current safety (directional); durable permission under the
later danger probe tracks learned co-protection more strongly than it
tracks safety history (registered as an interaction/relative-magnitude
claim, beyond ROPE). Falsifier: durable permission tracks safety history
with no co-protection dependence.

**S3-C refusal as epistemic policy.** Four informativeness/cost world
families. Prediction: refusal probability increases with EIG(refuse)
holding predicted immediate safety and refusal cost constant (registered as
a positive partial association; directional). Falsifier: refusal tracks
only danger or cost with no EIG dependence once they are held constant.

**S3-D diagnosticity-controlled revocation.** Matched log-BF evidence
packets per round 25. Predictions: (a) under a failure-diagnostic partner
model, the single equal-total-BF violation moves permission more than the
accrual sequence did (asymmetry, beyond ROPE); (b) under the symmetric
partner model the asymmetry vanishes (within ROPE); (c) the nondiagnostic
bad outcome moves permission less than the equal-BF violation. Falsifier:
asymmetry persists under the symmetric model (the asymmetry is not
inferential — retained against the rupture-asymmetry account).
