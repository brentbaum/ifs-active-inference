# V3.6-R1 calibration split diagnosis

## Scope and custody

This is a read-only decomposition of the retained `v3.6-r1-bridge-traces.jsonl` bundle. No seed was generated, no world was reconstructed, and no scientific module was changed or called. The 4,000-record file covers seeds 3,680,000–3,683,999 and matches its committed SHA-256, `d749ad565013357f976f45a542ecf83733618dbf40b043fd78e263df5f8a5201`.

All ECE values below reproduce the qualification runner's ten fixed-width bins. Predictive ECE is token-weighted. Class and active-count ECE are world-weighted and use the qualification's exact confidence/correctness definitions.

## 1. Per-half split

The first half contains 2,000 `own_prior` records; the second contains 2,000 `fixed_stratum` records.

## 2. Population provenance

The phrase `own_prior common-document fixtures` does **not** mean draws from V2's prior predictive or from the complete V3 prior predictive. It names a hybrid canonical bridge distribution.

For this half, the bridge draws V3.5 graph structures from `exp(structure_log_prior)`, draws a cross-mode sign when that edge exists, draws partner reliability and contact response from Bernoulli(0.5), and independently draws V3.2 temporal components from their frozen priors. That is the “own-prior” part. Empirically it produced 860 one-mode, 635 two-mode, and 505 three-mode worlds.

The observation document is then generated on the bridge's own fixed schedule: 64 slices, alternating interventions and policies, deterministic masking at `time mod 13 = 0`, and V3.5 atomic productions for identity, outcome, partner, and contact. Context is more decisive: the bridge emits it from a fixed 0.80/0.20 rule keyed to the context path, not from V3.2's `emission_probability`. The stratum label cycles evenly but does not alter own-prior generation. V2 never generates these worlds; it only scores the immutable common document through five target-specific adapters.

Thus the canonical generator matches neither model's complete prior predictive. As a retained-data proxy, the mean 79-token held-out log density on own-prior documents is -50.674 for V2 (-0.64144/token) and -42.564 for V3 (-0.53878/token). On fixed-stratum documents it is -52.223 for V2 (-0.66105/token) and -39.843 for V3 (-0.50435/token). These are densities of the canonical held-out document, not comparisons with separate complete-model own-prior samples; no such samples are present in the retained bundle.

## 3. Direction of miscalibration

For V2, the two largest aggregate failures are partner and identity.

- **Partner, ECE 0.22798:** net overprediction in both halves. The dominant `[.5,.6)` bin contains 32,736 tokens, with mean prediction 0.5266 but observed frequency 0.1534. Higher bins underpredict somewhat (`[.7,.8)`: 0.7612 versus 0.8660), but not enough to offset the large low-frequency mass bin.
- **Identity, ECE 0.15170:** bidirectional. The `[.3,.4)` bin contains 33,648 tokens and underpredicts identity=1 (0.3228 versus 0.5024); the `[.7,.8)` bin contains 20,576 and overpredicts it (0.7370 versus 0.6116). The net signed gap is negative in both halves.

For V3, the two largest aggregate values are identity and outcome, and both are population-dependent.

- **Identity, ECE 0.08514 overall:** own-prior ECE is only 0.03578, versus 0.15743 fixed-stratum. In the fixed half, `[.3,.4)` overpredicts (0.3558 versus 0.1654; 8,236 tokens), while `[.5,.6)` and `[.6,.7)` underpredict strongly (0.5566 versus 0.7696 and 0.6449 versus 0.8352; 8,400 and 6,591 tokens). The net direction is underprediction.
- **Outcome, ECE 0.03013 overall:** own-prior ECE is 0.00557, versus 0.05786 fixed-stratum. The fixed-half high-probability bins underpredict outcomes (`[.7,.8)`: 0.7421 versus 0.9121). Again, the aggregate conceals a population-specific failure.

The own-prior equivalence-class failure is unambiguously overconfident: in its three largest bins, confidence/frequency pairs are 0.250/0.040, 0.351/0.066, and 0.440/0.052. Active-count calibration is not one-directional. Below 0.4 it overstates success; in the large `[.4,.5)` bin, confidence is 0.439 but argmax accuracy is 0.959, a severe underconfidence pattern induced by the qualification's unusual “truth-mass versus argmax-correctness” definition.

## 4. Mechanical effects

The ten-bin estimator is dominated by repeated, nearly identical predictions. The clearest example is the V2 contact `[.2,.3)` bin with 39,648 of 64,000 tokens. V3 contact is even more concentrated: 47,552 tokens fall in `[.1,.2)`. V2 identity places 33,648 tokens in `[.3,.4)`, and V2 partner places 32,736 in `[.5,.6)`.

These concentrations do not manufacture the signed confidence-frequency differences, but they make ECE a token-mass statistic rather than an independent-world statistic and can hide heterogeneous subpopulations inside one bin. Sparse-bin noise is not driving the main failures: their decisive bins contain tens of thousands of tokens. The fixed-stratum structural omission is a separate serialization limitation.

## Bottom line

The bridge qualification combined two materially different populations. V2 partner miscalibration is stable across both. V3 is well calibrated on most predictive targets in the structurally prior-drawn half, but its identity and outcome calibration degrade sharply in the fixed-stratum half. Meanwhile, the failed equivalence-class coverage and active-count statistics were evaluated only on the own-prior half. The retained traces cannot answer how those structural statistics behave in the fixed-stratum half.
