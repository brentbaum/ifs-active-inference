> Conditional-randomization selectivity was considerably more stringent. It identified 23.3% of genuine histories and 28.3% of formed-organization histories, while remaining at 5.0–6.7% in marginal-preserving shuffled controls and zero in genuine single-regime and exact nuisance-family controls. Mean null excess was negative in both genuine populations. The construction therefore supports material context-indexed redescription and its predictive consequences, but not reliable identification of most individual finite histories beyond the signal already contained in cue-wise outcome and marker marginals.

Never claim: population-level selectivity established; most genuine histories distinguishable from chance; mean evidence exceeded the conditional null; formed organization prior-favored redescription; conditional randomization confirmed redescription. Distinguish four properties: five-family predictive adequacy (supported); material two-context structural use (supported); historical retention and present-context transfer (supported); conditional selectivity beyond preserved marginals (highly specific, incompletely powered, negative mean null excess).

Framing: state the limitation plainly (preregistered finite-history conditional-selectivity benchmarks not met) and present the methodological result as application-specific (cue-wise marginals retain context-generated signal; conditioning on them yields strong specificity but a null containing mixed-context ingredients) — not a general CRT theorem. Predictive adequacy, structural use, therapeutic consequence, and conditional detectability are different properties.
