## Rationale (ruling summary)
- This is not the criterion-invalidity category: the CRT is a high-specificity test with incomplete individual-world power, not an invalid instrument. The diagnosis does not establish an asymptotic ceiling below 0.60 (selectivity rises 0.20 → 0.35 → 0.44 with information; observed evidence grows faster than the null; the larger failure component is weaker realized evidence in nonselective worlds, not the null).
- The .60 floor was an uncalibrated but intelligible power target; the model did not meet it; that remains in the record. Its **role** is reclassified — a failed secondary performance benchmark, not a necessary condition for the claim that the model can infer and use a materially occupied context-indexed representation.
- A fifth successor designed after seeing 0.44 would create more concern about iterative criterion replacement than scientific value.

## Claim text (adopted)
Current gate-2-supported claim:
> The exact model recovered a materially occupied two-context representation in 92% of context-split worlds, while material redescription occurred in at most 1% of worlds generated by each of four nuisance families. A more stringent within-world conditional-randomization test showed no false selectivity in 400 nuisance-family worlds but detected selectivity in 44% of genuine context-split worlds at the frozen 96-slice information budget. Selective detection increased monotonically with history length. Thus the construction supports materially two-context redescription and highly specific, but incomplete, finite-history discrimination beyond cue-wise marginals.

Final claim if gates 3–5 and C-V24 retain the bridge results:
> Genuine then/now evidence can support a materially occupied context-indexed representation among live global, cue-local, drift, and change-point alternatives. In the integrated construction, that representation preserves the historical prediction while present-context identity-root revision transfers to untreated cues; fixing the root removes transfer. A stringent cue-marginal conditional-randomization test has high empirical specificity but limited individual-history power, so the result does not imply that every — or most — genuine context histories can be distinguished from chance alignment at a finite observation budget.

Never claim "the model selectively recovered context splits in most genuine worlds." Formed-P wording: "the redescription route can operate from a previously formed P organization" — not that formation prior-favors splitting (bank-vs-neutral audit found exact identity).

## Presentation rules
- Call the 0.44-vs-0.00 result a **specificity–power asymmetry**. Wilson intervals: 44/100 → [0.347, 0.538]; 0/400 → upper bound ≈ 0.0095. Do not call 0/400 "perfect calibration" — say no false selective classifications were observed in 400 nuisance worlds (Wilson upper 95% ≈ 0.95%); semantic calibration comes from the exchangeability proof.
- Compact figure: material and selective rates vs information (0.61/0.85/0.92 and 0.20/0.35/0.44); mean T0; mean Q95; contrast and marker accuracy for selective vs nonselective material worlds. Key sentence: "Nonselective material worlds combined weaker realized context evidence with a stronger conditional null. Approximately two thirds of the group separation in null excess came from lower observed evidence and one third from a higher null threshold."
- Frame as a limitation AND an application-specific methodological result (the exact decomposition of the conditioning validity–power tradeoff for context-indexed generative models) — not a newly discovered general theorem about CRTs.

## Permanent design lesson (adopted)
> Distinguish structural recovery, conditional selectivity, and finite-history power. A structure can be correctly inferred from a realized history even when that history does not clear a stringent conditional test after signal-bearing marginals are fixed.
