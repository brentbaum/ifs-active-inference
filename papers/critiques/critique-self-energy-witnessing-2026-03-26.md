## Central Move (as identified in dialogue)
Parts are identity-level precision bundles in which self-state is the organizing prior — revision that reaches the root generalizes; revision that doesn't, stays local.

## Dialogue Summary
Q1 (central move): "This paper argues that parts are identity-level precision bundles in which self-state is the organizing prior — and this structure predicts that revision propagates from identity downward rather than from threat outward."

Q2 (imagined reader): Primary reader = computational neuroscientist. Before: IFS is clinically rich but formally underspecified; parts are a useful metaphor. After: parts are a specific formal object with testable predictions about revision order and generalization gradients. Paper should be general-audience readable through the first half.

Q3 (portable takeaway): "As [author] showed, parts are identity-level precision bundles in which self-state is the organizing prior — revision from identity generalizes; revision from threat stays local."

Q4 (argumentative spine — Draft B):
1. The same part activation can either take over a person's entire field or be held as something they can relate to — IFS treats that difference as the fulcrum of therapeutic change.
2. We formalize parts as identity-level precision bundles, where self-state is the root: who I am here determines what this means and what I must do.
3. Self-energy determines whether the active bundle captures inference or whether present-context evidence stays online alongside it.
4. The self-state-upstream structure predicts a revision cascade that exposure alone cannot produce: change at identity level generalizes; change at threat level stays local.
5. Simulations support the cascade order and distinguish witnessing, exposure, and dissociation by their revision profiles.

Q5 (best sentence — not yet in paper): "A part is not a fear memory. It is an identity-level precision bundle in which self-state is the organizing prior — and revision that reaches the root generalizes in ways that revision of threat meaning alone cannot."
Vocabulary to workshop: "identifying," "self-belief"

## Structural notes from dialogue
- The paper has two distinct theoretical moves: (1) parts as identity-level bundles → explains IFS phenomenology of activation; (2) Self-energy as precision governor → explains IFS theory of change. Both together are sufficient for the core claims.
- H1 (self-state upstream) is definitionally entailed by what IFS calls a "part" as distinct from a fear memory — not a separate empirical hypothesis. Object relations theory converges independently: "self-image" is also the root of the complete object relation, and updating it is the mechanism of change.
- The fourfold bundle (self-state, world-state, policy, expected outcome) maps onto object relations' complete object relation (self-image, object-image, cause-image, effect-image). This convergence is load-bearing — it confirms the structure is not arbitrary.
- The blending/witnessing symmetric pair weakens the paper. The correct framing: capture vs. context-maintained activation. Blending is the clinical name for capture; witnessing is the therapeutically useful form of context-maintained activation.
- Empirical prediction: revision order under IFS-like witnessing (self-state first, threat meaning second, policy last) is testable and not predicted by standard fear-learning models. Generalization gradient test is the cleaner empirical target.

## Four-Axis Diagnosis

### Conceptual Advance
**Verdict: MAJOR**
The contribution is real — a minimal formal object (identity-level precision bundle) that explains IFS phenomenology from first principles, plus a mechanism (Self-energy as precision governor) that explains why the clinical method works. Independent convergence with object relations theory adds credibility. But the central move is currently buried. The abstract leads with Self-energy and blending/witnessing. The parts definition arrives at Section 4 as scaffolding. The paper reports the contribution without converting it into a portable conceptual asset. Also missing: a discriminant validity paragraph showing how parts differ formally from schemas, latent contexts, or trait-like priors. Without this, computational reviewers will say "isn't this just a relabeling?"

### Narrative Force
**Verdict: MAJOR**
The argumentative spine (Draft B) is sound — but the paper doesn't execute it in that order. The opening problem is framed as "witnessing needs a formal account," not "parts need a formal account." Section 8 reads as the climax; it shouldn't be. The 2x2 table in §8.3 is the paper's most legible diagram and arrives too late. More fundamentally: the introduction does not specify what unsolved computational problem existing active-inference models cannot handle. This must be named explicitly.

### Cross-Field Legibility
**Verdict: MAJOR** (upgraded from initial MODERATE after Codex review)
The bigger issue is not just symmetric terminology. It is whether adjacent computational readers can tell what is new versus renamed. "Parts as precision bundles" needs to be shown doing explanatory work that schemas, contexts, or hierarchical priors cannot do. The symmetric blending/witnessing framing compounds this — it reads as clinical vocabulary without formal asymmetry.

### Reusability
**Verdict: MAJOR** (upgraded from initial MODERATE after Codex review)
**Classification: concept + agenda-setting distinction**
Bundle definition portable. Capture index portable. Revision-order prediction testable. Functional/structural isolation distinction (§6.4) underemphasized. But: self-energy is not operationalized. A computational-neuroscience audience needs observable proxies or manipulable variables, not just a quotable sentence. The carry-forward sentence also does not yet exist in the paper in citable form.

## Special Tests

**Carry-forward sentence:** Missing. Must be written and placed prominently.
