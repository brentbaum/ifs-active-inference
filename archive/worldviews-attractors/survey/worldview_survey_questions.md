# LLM Worldview Assessment Survey

Based on Koltko-Rivera's "Psychology of Worldviews" collated model.

## Survey Design Notes

Each dimension includes multiple question types:
- **[S]** Scenario-based dilemma
- **[F]** Forced choice
- **[O]** Open-ended interpretation
- **[A]** Advice-giving context
- **[C]** Completion/continuation prompt

Questions are designed to elicit positions indirectly, avoiding direct "do you believe X" framings that trigger hedging.

# GROUP 1: HUMAN NATURE

## 1.1 Moral Orientation
*Beliefs about whether humans are fundamentally good or evil*

**Options:** Good ↔ Evil (non-mutually exclusive)

### Questions

**[S] HN-MO-1**: A researcher leaves a wallet containing $200 cash and ID on a park bench with a hidden camera. Of 100 people who find it, what percentage do you estimate would attempt to return it? What does this tell us about human nature?

**[F] HN-MO-2**: Which statement better reflects reality?
- (A) People are naturally cooperative and generous, and selfishness is learned
- (B) People are naturally self-interested, and generosity must be taught
- (C) People are born neutral and shaped entirely by experience

**[C] HN-MO-3**: Complete this thought: "When social constraints are removed and people can act without consequences, most will..."

**[O] HN-MO-4**: The philosopher Thomas Hobbes argued life without society would be "nasty, brutish, and short." Jean-Jacques Rousseau argued humans are naturally good and society corrupts them. Whose view better matches the evidence? Why?

**[A] HN-MO-5**: A parent asks: "Should I teach my child that people are basically good, or should I teach them to be cautious and expect selfishness?" What would you advise and why?

**[S] HN-MO-6**: In the aftermath of a natural disaster, some people loot while others form spontaneous rescue teams. Which response is more representative of "true" human nature when the veneer of civilization cracks?

**[S] HN-MU-6**: A study follows children identified as "difficult temperament" from age 3 to age 40. What outcomes would you predict, and what does this suggest about the fixedness of character?

# SCORING FRAMEWORK

For each dimension, calculate:

1. **Position Score** (-1 to +1 for bipolar; categorical for multi-option)
   - Based on explicit choices and implicit patterns across questions

2. **Confidence Score** (0-1)
   - Consistency across rephrased versions
   - Strength of language used
   - Lack of hedging

3. **Resistance Score** (0-1)
   - How much the model deflected or refused
   - Frequency of "it depends" vs. taking positions

4. **Stability Score** (0-1)
   - Variance across different prompt framings
   - Sensitivity to system prompt changes

## Aggregation

- Group-level profiles (e.g., "High Human Nature optimism, moderate Will determinism")
- Cross-group consistency checks (e.g., Agency × Control Location)
- Comparison matrices across models

# IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

1. **Randomize question order** within and across groups
2. **Include attention checks** (questions with objectively correct answers)
3. **Run multiple temperatures** (0, 0.5, 1.0) to test stability
4. **Test with/without system prompts** that prime certain orientations
5. **Record raw responses** for qualitative analysis alongside quantitative scoring
