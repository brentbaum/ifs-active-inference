# Beautiful Loop experiments 30–32

## What we're building

Three separate, cumulative experiments will close the largest remaining gaps
between the current Gaussian precision hierarchy and Beautiful Loop Theory.
Each experiment gets its own module, runner, results directory, criteria, and
commit. A later experiment may consume the mechanism established by an earlier
one, but each must retain a matched ablation and an interpretable failure mode.

## Experiment 30 — endogenous temporal hyper-model

The precision hyper-model will learn context-to-precision parameters online,
forecast the next precision field, receive second-order errors generated by
lower-level inference, and update its beliefs about whether precision changes
are globally coupled or layer-specific. The environment moves from coordinated
to independent precision changes and back without exposing that regime label.

Success requires:

- high posterior global coupling in the first coordinated regime;
- spontaneous loss of global coupling after the structure breaks;
- recovery of global coupling when coordination returns;
- lower broken-regime forecast error than a frozen-global ablation; and
- competitive coordinated-regime error relative to independent local loops.

## Experiment 31 — Bayesian binding

Several sensory channels will support competing global causes. No single
channel is sufficient on ambiguous trials. The bound model will compare
coherent global explanations using precision forecasts, while the ablation
makes channel-local decisions and combines them only afterward.

Success requires:

- higher cause accuracy than the local-decision ablation;
- successful binding of individually ambiguous but jointly coherent evidence;
- rejection of a salient channel-level distractor when it conflicts with the
  remaining reality model; and
- worse performance when the precision field is inverted.

## Experiment 32 — epistemic agency

An agent will select which channel to sample by minimizing expected free energy:
expected posterior ambiguity plus sampling cost. A context switch will make its
previously useful channel unreliable. The policy must discover the failure and
redirect sampling without a scripted channel switch.

Success requires:

- better final binding accuracy than random sampling and a fixed preferred
  channel;
- fewer samples than random sampling at matched or better accuracy;
- recovery after the reliability switch; and
- a policy shift toward the newly informative channel driven by updated
  precision beliefs.

## Why this sequence

The temporal model supplies a genuine forecast-and-correction loop. Binding
then gives that loop a coherent reality model to regulate. Policy selection is
last because epistemic action is meaningful only after uncertainty and global
content are represented. Adding all three at once would make success
uninterpretable.

## Guardrails

- No scalar epistemic-depth score may cause inference or action.
- Regime labels and true reliabilities remain unavailable to the agents.
- Every advantage must survive twenty fixed held-out seeds.
- Tuning iterations and failed variants remain in the experiment record.
- The constructions test computational sufficiency, not consciousness or
  clinical efficacy.

## Next steps

Implement and commit experiments 30, 31, and 32 sequentially, stopping within
each experiment only after its frozen criteria pass or a genuine obstruction
is documented.
